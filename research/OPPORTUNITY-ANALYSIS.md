# Opportunity Analysis — Phases 2 through 5

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Founder profile this is scored against:** Nigeria-based, year-2 BSc Economics student. Sellable skill = research/analysis. Willing to do high-volume cold outreach. No network, no audience. Under $100 capital. 20–40 hrs/week. Needs first revenue in 30–60 days. Target $500–1,000/month profit within 6 months. Domiciliary account; needs a card-acceptance layer. Good English, wants AI polish. Decent with tools, learns fast.

---

## 1. The three market facts that shaped everything below

These came from research, not intuition. Each one killed or reshaped a candidate idea.

**Fact 1 — Raw list-building is dead as a standalone service.**
AI has commoditized data compilation. Agencies can run Clay, Apollo and an LLM themselves. What still gets paid for is *judgment*: research insight, qualification, and validation. Any offer whose deliverable is "a list of names and emails" is a race to zero. Any offer whose deliverable is "a list where every row carries a verified, dated, cited reason to act" is still a human job.

**Fact 2 — Agencies are the most reachable B2B buyer on earth, and they buy delivery capacity.**
Agencies that outsource 40–60% of service delivery grow 2.3× faster than peers at 18–22% higher margins. They are publicly listed in directories, they advertise, they are contactable, and critically — they cannot object to cold outreach, because cold outreach is what they sell. They also resell what they buy, which makes them white-label buyers who do not care where the founder lives.

**Fact 3 — Cold email math is unforgiving, and it sets the whole plan's scale.**
2026 benchmarks: 3.43% average reply rate, 5–10% is good, 8–12% is elite, and advanced personalization reaches ~18%. Lists under 50 recipients average 5.8% vs 2.1% for large blasts. **Implication: volume is not the lever — relevance is.** Every plan below is sized on a conservative 5–8% reply rate, not a hoped-for 15%.

## 3. The 25 opportunities (Phase 4)

Scores in Section 4. `TTFR` = time to first revenue.

### Fast cash-flow / service

**O1 — Trigger-Qualified Prospect Research (white-label for B2B agencies)**
Deliverable is not a list; it is a list where each row carries a documented, dated buying signal with a source URL (new funding, new exec hire, a job posting implying a gap, tech-stack change, expansion, competitor complaints).
- *Customer:* B2B lead-gen / outbound / SDR agencies, 2–15 staff, US/UK/CA/AU.
- *Problem:* P1. Campaign relevance decays; junior VAs produce volume without judgment.
- *Offer:* $147 for a 50-row pilot pack → $450–650/mo for 200 rows/mo.
- *Currently:* Apollo exports + a VA, or Clay credits.
- *Why better:* the signal + citation is the product. AI does volume; the human does the judgment call on whether a signal is real.
- *Startup cost:* ~$20. *Margin:* 90%+. *TTFR:* 14–30 days. *Difficulty:* Low-medium. *Competition:* High at the commodity end, thin at the qualified end. *Scalability:* Medium (labour-linked until productized). *Recurring:* Strong (monthly refresh). *AI leverage:* Very high. *Tech:* None initially — Sheets + AI. *Distribution:* Cold email/LinkedIn where the outreach itself is the demo. *Biggest risk:* buyer decides Clay + AI is good enough.

**O2 — Bid & Tender Concierge (RFP monitoring + human qualification)**
Monitor public procurement portals for one client's exact capability profile; deliver a weekly shortlist with a go/no-go recommendation and deadline.
- *Customer:* SMB contractors, IT services firms, consultancies that bid on public work.
- *Problem:* P2. *Offer:* $250–400/mo retainer. *Currently:* manual checks or $600–900/mo software. *Why better:* undercuts software on price and beats it on relevance because a human filters out noise.
- *Startup cost:* ~$15 (portals are free). *Margin:* 90%. *TTFR:* 30–60 days. *Difficulty:* Medium. *Competition:* Low at this price point. *Scalability:* Medium-high. *Recurring:* Very strong — inherently subscription. *AI leverage:* High. *Biggest risk:* long sales cycle with conservative buyers.

**O3 — Recruiting Sourcing Support (white-label for boutique recruiters)**
Deliver 25–40 qualified, contact-enriched candidate profiles per open role.
- *Customer:* Independent recruiters and 2–10 person staffing firms.
- *Offer:* $200–350 per role, or $800/mo for 4 roles. *Currently:* they do it themselves at night. *Why better:* their hourly value is in closing, not searching; 15–25% placement fees make $300 trivial.
- *Startup cost:* ~$30. *Margin:* 85%. *TTFR:* 21–40 days. *Recurring:* Good. *Biggest risk:* quality ceiling without paid LinkedIn Recruiter access.

**O4 — Investor List Research for founders raising**
Thesis-matched investor lists with partner-level targeting and a "why they'd care" line per row.
- *Offer:* $250–500 per raise. *Recurring:* Poor (one-off). *TTFR:* 21 days. *Biggest risk:* pre-seed founders are cash-poor and the purchase never repeats.

**O5 — CRM Data Hygiene & Enrichment sprints**
De-dupe, verify, re-title, and re-enrich a rotting CRM.
- *Offer:* $200–600 per sprint, quarterly. *Margin:* 90%. *Biggest risk:* low urgency; easy to defer forever.

**O6 — Off-Market Deal Sourcing for search funders / small acquirers**
Build lists of owner-operated businesses matching acquisition criteria, with owner-age/succession signals.
- *Offer:* $500–1,500/mo. *WTP:* very high (buyers have capital). *Biggest risk:* small buyer pool, hard to reach, high trust bar for a student.

**O7 — Competitive Intelligence Briefs (monthly)**
Monthly brief on a client's 5 competitors: pricing changes, hires, launches, ad copy, review themes.
- *Offer:* $300–500/mo. *Recurring:* strong. *Biggest risk:* ROI hard to prove, first thing cut in a downturn.

**O8 — Grant & Funder Prospect Research for nonprofits**
Funder shortlists with fit reasoning, ask size, deadline.
- *Offer:* $400–900 per report (market comparable: $1,500). *Biggest risk:* slow, credential-sensitive buyers; a foreign student faces real friction here.

**O9 — White-Label SEO / Content Research for marketing agencies**
Keyword + SERP gap + brief packages delivered under the agency's brand.
- *Offer:* $50–120 per brief, $500–1,200/mo. *Biggest risk:* the most crowded, most AI-commoditized category on this list.

**O10 — Podcast & Newsletter Sponsor/Guest Research**
Qualified target lists with contact paths and a fit rationale.
- *Offer:* $200–400/mo. *Biggest risk:* thin, fragmented buyer market.

### Local / offline (Nigeria)

**O11 — Nigerian SME lead lists & market-entry research** — naira pricing caps this at a fraction of USD work. Same labour, ~20% of the revenue.
**O12 — Import/export supplier verification for Nigerian importers** — real pain (fraud is common), but trust-heavy and hard to sell cold as a student.
**O13 — Local business digitization (Google Business Profile, listings)** — low ticket, high foot-work, does not use the research skill.

### Digital products / information

**O14 — Niche directory as a paid database** — build once, sell access. Slow to revenue; needs an audience first. Fails the 60-day test.
**O15 — "Signal playbook" info product** — teaching the trigger-research method. Cannot sell credibly before doing the work. Correct *second* act, wrong first.
**O16 — Curated newsletter (funded companies / new tenders) with sponsors** — 6–12 months to meaningful revenue. Fails the 60-day test but is an excellent distribution asset.

### Data / AI-enabled

**O17 — Trigger-monitoring micro-SaaS** — the productized form of O1/O2. Correct destination, wrong starting point: building before selling is the classic failure.
**O18 — AI research-ops support for boutique consultancies** — good money, but the buyer requires demonstrated pedigree.
**O19 — Review-mining insight reports for e-commerce brands** — interesting, but buyers are hard to identify and the value is diffuse.
**O20 — Compliance/regulatory monitoring briefs** — high value, high stakes, and a wrong answer is a liability. Not a beginner's category.

### Marketplace / arbitrage / other

**O21 — Upwork/Fiverr research gigs** — not a business; a *channel*. Useful as a proof-and-review farm, not a destination.
**O22 — Reselling offshore delivery capacity (Nigerian VAs) to Western agencies** — real arbitrage, but it is an operational/HR business, not a research business, and it requires cash for payroll float.
**O23 — E-commerce product & supplier research** — crowded and commoditized on Fiverr.
**O24 — Academic literature review support** — flagged deliberately: the version students actually get asked for is contract cheating. The legitimate version (literature searches for professional researchers) is a thin, low-paying market. **Recommend avoiding entirely** — reputational and account-ban risk outweighs the money.
**O25 — Relocation/visa research service** — B2C, emotionally charged, low WTP, high support burden.

## 4. Scoring model (Phase 5)

### Weights, and why

Weights are set against **this founder's actual constraints**, not against an abstract "good business." A 60-day revenue deadline and $100 of capital mean speed and acquisition dominate; defensibility is deliberately down-weighted because an undefended business earning $800/month beats a defensible one earning $0.

**This is a stated bias, not a hidden one.** If the goal were $10k/month over 24 months, Defensibility and Scalability would carry 3–4× these weights and O2/O17 would win outright.

| Criterion | Weight | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| Speed to first revenue | 12 | Hard 60-day deadline |
| Ease of customer acquisition | 12 | Cold start, no network, no audience |
| Founder suitability | 11 | Research skill is narrow; must be used directly |
| Demand | 10 | Must already exist, not be created |
| Willingness to pay | 10 | Budget must already be allocated |
| Startup cost (low = high score) | 8 | Under $100 is a hard ceiling |
| Ease of delivery | 8 | One person, learning on the job |

| Profit margin | 6 | Most info-services are high margin anyway |
| Competition (low = high score) | 4 | Matters, but beatable with positioning |
| Scalability | 4 | Not the constraint at this income target |
| Market size | 3 | Only ~3 clients needed; TAM is irrelevant |
| Defensibility | 3 | Deliberately low — see note above |
| AI leverage | 1 | A tool, not a thesis |

### Category winners

| Question | Answer | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **First $100** | O1 — pilot pack | A $147 one-off an agency can approve without a meeting. Cash in ~14–21 days. |
| **First $1,000/month** | O1 — 2 retainers | Two clients at ~$500/mo. ~30 hrs/month of delivery. Reachable by day 60–90. |
| **Path to $10,000/month** | O2, or O1 productized | Requires either subscription mechanics or a small Nigerian delivery team. Labour-linked O1 caps out around $4–5k solo. |
| **Best long-term company** | O2 → O17 | Tender/signal monitoring accumulates proprietary data and client-specific criteria. That is a real moat; a list is not. |
| **Lowest risk** | O1 | Agencies always need delivery capacity. If one churns, the offer still works on the next. |
| **Near-zero capital** | O1 | Genuinely runnable on ~$20: a domain and free tiers. |

### Deliberately rejected, with reasons

- **O17 micro-SaaS first** — building software before a paying customer is the single most common way this fails. Software comes at Phase 11, only if manual delivery proves demand.
- **O24 academic support** — the demand is real and mostly for work that is academic misconduct. Not worth the risk to a student's own standing.
- **O11–O13 local Nigerian services** — same labour, roughly a fifth of the revenue. The arbitrage runs the other way; use it.
- **O16 newsletter** — right asset, wrong sequence. It becomes the inbound engine in month 4+, once there is something to write from.
