# SALES.md — Phase 9: Sales System

**Last updated:** 2026-08-29 · **Context:** no commercial track record, 100% cold acquisition
(D-011), first sale target $100 (D-012).

**Governing principle:** you are not persuading anyone. You are finding the small number of people
who already have the problem, and disqualifying everyone else fast. Chasing an unqualified
prospect costs more than losing them.

---

## 1. Qualification criteria

Score every prospect before spending time. **Three or more "no" answers = disqualify and move on.**

| # | Criterion | Qualifying signal | Disqualifying signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Named decision** | "We're deciding whether to enter Senegal" | "Just curious about the region" |
| 2 | **Deadline** | Within 8 weeks | "Sometime next year" |
| 3 | **Budget authority** | Can approve $200–$2,000 alone | "I'd have to run it past procurement" |
| 4 | **Answerable with secondary data** | Market structure, regulation, competitors, sizing | Needs surveys, fieldwork, or interviews |
| 5 | **Consequence of not knowing** | Real money or a real deadline at stake | Nothing changes either way |
| 6 | **Treats research as a purchase** | Asks what it costs | Asks if you'll do a free sample first |

**Hard disqualifiers, no exceptions:** wants free work "to see the quality" (the published sample
is the free work) · needs primary fieldwork · wants it in under 3 days · opens by negotiating
price before scope · is a student, job seeker, or pre-funding founder.

**Declining out-of-scope work is a selling behaviour.** "That needs fieldwork, which I don't do —
you'd want a firm with people on the ground" builds more credibility than any pitch.

## 2. Discovery call framework — 20 minutes

Not 45. A short call signals you know what you're doing and respects a buyer's time.

| Min | Stage | What you actually say |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Frame** | "Thanks for the time. I've got about twenty minutes — I'd like to understand what you're deciding, and then I'll tell you honestly whether I can help. If I can't, I'll say so." |
| 1–6 | **Context** | "What are you working on in [market]?" · "How did this come onto your desk?" · "What do you already know, and where does it run out?" |
| 6–11 | **Pain & cost** | "What happens if you make this decision without the information?" · "What have you tried so far?" · "How much time has your team already spent on this?" |
| 11–14 | **Decision process** | "When do you need to have decided?" · "Who else weighs in?" · "Have you bought research like this before?" |
| 14–17 | **Fit — including honest no** | "Here's what I can answer with institutional data, and here's what I can't." |
| 17–20 | **Price and next step** | State the number. Stop talking. |

**The two most important behaviours on the call:**

1. **Ask, then be quiet.** The prospect should talk for about 70% of it. Every silence you fill is
   information you don't get.
2. **State the price out loud, then say nothing** until they respond. Do not soften it, do not
   justify it, do not immediately offer a discount. The pause is uncomfortable and it is the
   single highest-value habit in this document.

## 3. Sales script — the moments that decide the outcome

**Opening (they came from a DM):**
> "You mentioned you're looking at [market] and running into the French-language sources. Tell me
> what you're actually trying to decide."

**Transition to the offer:**
> "So if I've got this right: you need to know [the question] by [date], and the blocker is that
> the credible data is in French and scattered across BCEAO and the national statistics office.
> Is that fair? — Right. That's exactly what I do."

**Presenting the deliverable — describe the object, not the process:**
> "You'd get a [5–8 / 15–25] page brief in [5 / 10] working days. Every figure carries a named
> institutional source, so your team can check any of it. It states what the data can't tell you
> as clearly as what it can. And because your local partner is in Cotonou, I'd deliver it in
> French as well — same document, written natively, not translated."

**The price, delivered flat:**
> "That's $[X], half to start and half on delivery."
>
> *(Then stop. Say nothing. Let them speak first.)*

**Closing:**
> "If that works, I'll send a one-page scope today and an invoice for the deposit. I'd start
> Monday and you'd have it by the [date]."

## 4. Objection handling

**"You're a student."** — the one you will hear, and the one that must be answered without flinching.

> "I am — second year, economics. That's why my published sample is out there in full: read the
> methodology and the limitations sections and judge the work rather than the CV. It's also why
> I'm $800 instead of $15,000. What I actually bring is that I read the French primary sources
> natively and I'm in the region. If you want a name on the cover for the board, you should hire a
> firm. If you want the question answered properly, I'm a better use of the budget."

**Never** get defensive, apologise, or oversell to compensate. Own it, then redirect to the work.

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Compared to what — a consultancy, or doing it in-house? If it's in-house, how many of your
> team's hours would this take?" *(Then, if genuinely budget-constrained: offer the smaller tier.
> Never discount the same scope. Reduce the scope or hold the price.)*

**"We can do this internally."**
> "You probably can. The question is whether it's the best use of the week it takes — and whether
> anyone on the team reads French well enough to work through BCEAO circulars and INStaD releases.
> If yes, do it internally. Genuinely."

**"Send me a proposal and I'll review it."**
> "Happy to. Before I do — what would need to be in it for you to say yes? I'd rather write the
> one you can act on than a generic one." *(An unanswerable version of this question means they
> are not a buyer. Downgrade the prospect.)*

**"How do I know the quality will be good?"**
> "You don't have to guess — the full 14-page sample is public, sources and all. And the first
> tier is refundable: if the snapshot isn't useful, you don't pay for it."

**"Can you do it cheaper?"**
> "I can do it smaller. The snapshot answers one question for $200 instead of the full brief. Same
> standard, narrower scope." *(Reduce scope, never price. Discounting the first sale sets your
> rate permanently.)*

**"We need it in three days."**
> "I can do a snapshot in three days. A full brief in three days would be a worse document than
> you deserve, and I'd rather tell you that now."

**"Let me discuss internally and get back to you."**
> "Of course. What's your read on how it'll land? And when should I check back — is Thursday
> reasonable?" *(Always leave with a specific date. "I'll follow up" is where deals die.)*

## 5. Proposal template — one page, sent within 24 hours

```
SCOPE OF WORK — [Client] · [Date]

THE QUESTION
[Their question, in their words. This is the most important line in the document.]

WHAT YOU RECEIVE
· [N]-page brief covering: [3–5 specific sections]
· Every figure sourced to a named institution
· Sourced data appendix
· [French version, written natively]
· One revision round · 30-minute walkthrough call

WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER
[Be explicit. Naming the limits is what makes the rest credible.]

TIMELINE
Start: [date] · Mid-point check-in: [date] · Delivery: [date]

INVESTMENT
$[X] — 50% to begin, 50% on delivery
[Tier 1 only: full refund if the brief isn't useful to you.]

TO PROCEED
Reply "confirmed" and I'll send the deposit invoice.
```

No cover letter. No company boilerplate. No stock photography. One page, their question at the top.

## 6. Follow-up sequence

| When | Message |
|---|---|
| **Day 2** | "Sent the scope over Monday — any questions on it?" |
| **Day 7** | Value, not chasing: "Came across [relevant item] on [their market] — thought of your question." |
| **Day 14** | "Is this still live on your side, or has it moved down the list? Either is fine — I just don't want to keep pinging you." |
| **Day 21** | **The close-out:** "I'll close this out for now — if it comes back around, you know where I am." |

That last message closes more deals than the first three combined. It removes pressure, and people
who genuinely wanted it reply immediately.

**Then stop.** Four touches maximum. Chasing beyond that costs reputation for a deal already lost.

## 7. Closing and handoff to delivery

1. Verbal or written yes
2. One-page scope confirmed in writing — **scope in writing before work starts, always**
3. Deposit invoice sent (see D-005 — the rail must work before you get here)
4. **Work begins when money moves.** No exceptions, no "I'll start while the transfer clears."
5. Calendar the mid-point check-in immediately
6. Deliver → walkthrough → **ask for the testimonial the same day**
7. 30 days later: offer the quarterly refresh or the retainer (E6)

## 8. Metrics to track — one spreadsheet, updated daily

| Metric | Definition | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Outreach sent | Invitations + first messages | The input you control. Log every one. |
| Acceptance rate | Invites accepted ÷ sent | <20% → profile or targeting is wrong |
| Reply rate | Replies ÷ accepted | <10% → message is wrong |
| Qualified rate | Qualified ÷ replies | <20% → targeting is wrong |
| Call booking rate | Calls ÷ qualified | <40% → offer is unclear |
| Close rate | Customers ÷ calls | <20% → price, proof, or qualification |
| Average order value | Revenue ÷ projects | Must rise with every delivery |
| CAC (in hours) | Hours spent acquiring ÷ customers won | The metric that tells you when to raise prices |
| LTV | Revenue per client across all purchases | Retainer target: 6+ months |

**Diagnostic rule — fix in funnel order, one thing at a time.** A low close rate is usually not a
closing problem; it is unqualified prospects reaching the call. Fix acceptance before reply, reply
before qualification, qualification before closing. Changing two things at once tells you nothing.

## 9. Weekly sales review — Friday, written down

1. Outreach sent · replies · calls · proposals · revenue. **Numbers, not impressions.**
2. What did prospects say — **verbatim quotes**? The exact words buyers use become next week's
   outreach copy and next month's post topics.
3. Which objection came up most?
4. Which prospect did I waste time on, and what was the signal I ignored?
5. One change for next week. **One.**
