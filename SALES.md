## Qualification — the 4 gates

A prospect is qualified only if all four are true. Disqualify fast; a wasted hour on a bad fit is an hour not spent on a good one.

1. **Has paying clients now.** No clients, no budget, no purchase.
2. **Runs outbound campaigns.** If they only do inbound/SEO, the pain doesn't exist.
3. **One decision-maker reachable.** If it needs a committee, walk away.
4. **Currently spends *something* on research** — a VA, Apollo, Clay, or their own hours. We are a substitution, not a new budget line.

**Instant disqualifiers:** asks for free work twice · wants to pay in equity or "exposure" · haggles before seeing the deliverable · needs a contract review for $147.

## The core mechanic: proof-first outreach

We never ask for attention. We deliver value before the first reply.

Standard cold email says *"I can do X for you."* → 3.43% average reply.
Ours says *"I already did X. Here it is. Here are two more like it."*

This is the entire strategy. It solves the credential problem, the location problem, and the trust problem simultaneously, because **finished work is not an argument — it cannot be disputed.**

### How to make it economical at volume

Researching 3 unique prospects per agency, from scratch, costs ~30 minutes each. At 200 agencies that is 100 hours. Not viable.

**The fix — build the pool once, distribute it many times:**
1. Build one pool of ~120 trigger-qualified companies that visibly need outbound help (hiring an SDR, recently funded, publicly struggling with pipeline). ~10–12 hours with AI assistance.
2. Assign each agency 3 rows from the pool that match *their* stated specialty. Never send the same 3 to two agencies.
3. Per-agency cost drops to **3–4 minutes**: pick the rows, write one specific line about their agency, send.

The pool is reusable inventory. This is what makes 200 personalized touches possible in a week rather than a month.

### Email v1 — cold, proof-first (target: 50–125 words)

> **Subject:** 3 companies hiring SDRs this week
>
> Hi {FirstName},
>
> Saw {Agency} works with {their niche} — so I pulled three companies that just showed a buying signal in that space:
>
> • **{Company A}** — posted an SDR role 6 days ago ({link})
> • **{Company B}** — raised a Series A on {date} ({link})
> • **{Company C}** — new VP Sales started this month ({link})
>
> Yours to use, no strings.
>
> I build these lists — every row with the signal, the date and the source. If it's useful I'll send 50 for one of your clients' ICPs.
>
> Worth a look?
>
> {Name}

**Why it works:** value lands before any ask · the links make it verifiable in ten seconds · "no strings" removes the trap feeling · the ask is tiny ("worth a look?" not "book a call").

### LinkedIn — connection note (300 char limit)

> Hi {FirstName} — I research buying signals for outbound agencies. Pulled 3 companies in {niche} showing signals this week, happy to send them over, no pitch. Thought {Agency} might find them useful.

### Follow-up sequence (email)

| Day | Message |
|---|---|
| 0 | Proof-first email above |
| 3 | "One more that came up: {Company D} — {signal}, {link}. Still happy to run a full 50 if useful." |
| 7 | "Last one from me — curious, how are you sourcing prospects for {client type} right now? Genuinely asking; it shapes what I build." |
| 21 | Fresh signals, no pitch. Restart the relationship. |

Three touches then stop. A fourth pitch annoys; a fresh gift in three weeks does not.

## Discovery questions

Ask these when someone replies. Listen for the *cost* of the current situation, not the features they want.

1. How are you sourcing prospects for clients right now?
2. Who does that work — you, a VA, or a tool?
3. How long does one client's monthly list take?
4. What percentage of a finished list do you consider genuinely usable?
5. What happens when a campaign underperforms — do you blame the list or the copy?
6. Have you used a research vendor before? What went wrong?
7. If research took zero hours, how many more clients could you take?

**Question 7 is the money question.** It converts our service from a cost into their growth constraint. Their own answer sells the retainer.

## Call structure (20 minutes)

| Min | Phase | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | "What made you reply?" | They state the pain themselves — better than any pitch |
| 2–8 | Discovery (above) | Quantify hours and money currently lost |
| 8–12 | Walk through the sample | Show, never describe. Screen-share the actual file. |
| 12–15 | The offer | "$147, 50 rows, one ICP, five days. If it's not usable, don't pay." |
| 15–18 | Objections | See CUSTOMER.md map |
| 18–20 | Close + logistics | Payment link, ICP definition, delivery date |

**Never present a slide deck. Present the file.**

## Proposal template (email, not a PDF)

> **What I'll deliver:** 50 companies matching {ICP}, each with a verified buying signal from the last 30 days, the signal date, a source link, and the best contact with title and email.
> **Format:** Google Sheet, yours to keep.
> **Turnaround:** 5 business days from ICP confirmation.
> **Price:** $147.
> **Guarantee:** if fewer than 40 of the 50 rows are usable, you don't pay.
> **Next step:** confirm the ICP and I'll start today.

The guarantee costs almost nothing (a 40/50 hit rate is achievable) and removes the entire risk of buying from an unknown vendor.

## Closing

Ask once, plainly, then stop talking: **"Want me to run the first 50?"** Silence after the ask is the close. Do not fill it.

## Referral request — after a successful delivery

> "Glad it worked. Quick ask — do you know one other agency owner running outbound who'd want the same thing? Happy to send them 3 free signals the same way I did for you."

Ask only after delivering something they visibly liked. Never before.
