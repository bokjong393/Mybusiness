## Post 2 — the value thread (day 10)

**Where:** X/Twitter thread, LinkedIn as one post. **No link in the thread —
put it in a reply.**

```
A client says "that's above our budget."

Most freelancers immediately drop 30%. That's the wrong move,
and here's what to do instead. 🧵

1/ First: "too expensive" is 5 different problems wearing the same coat.

- They have no budget
- They don't see the value
- They're comparing you to someone cheaper
- They're negotiating out of habit
- They're stalling for someone else's approval

You cannot answer until you know which one it is.

2/ You diagnose it from their exact words.

"That's above our budget" → they have a number. Ask what it is.
"Why is it this much?" → value problem. You explain, you don't discount.
"Can you do better?" → reflex. Hold firm, politely. They expect you to fold.

3/ The rule that changes everything:

NEVER reduce the price for the same scope.

Reduce the scope for a lower price. Every single time.

Same price, less work → your rate is intact.
Lower price, same work → you just taught them your rate was fake.

4/ What to say when the budget is real:

"I can work to [their number]. At that level I'd do [phase 1 only],
which gets you [specific outcome]. [Phase 2] stays on the table for later.
Does that work?"

You kept your rate. They kept their budget. Nobody lost.

5/ And when they won't move at all, the walk-away line:

"I don't think I'm the right fit at that budget, and I'd rather tell you
now than do a worse job than you deserve. If it changes, I'm here."

Say it warmly. Mean it. About 1 in 5 come back with more money.
```

**Reply with:** `I built a tool that does this diagnosis for you in about 30 seconds. Free tier, no signup: [LINK]`

**Why it works:** it is genuinely useful with zero purchase. The link in a reply
keeps the algorithm from suppressing it, and it makes the post shareable by
people who would never share an ad.

## Rules that keep this working

1. **Never post a number you did not measure.** Not once.
2. **Give the whole answer for free.** People buy the tool because the free
   advice was good, not because you withheld it.
3. **Reply to everything on day one.** Speed of reply is your only advantage
   over a funded competitor.
4. **Do not argue with critics in public.** "Fair — what would you change?"
   Then actually change it, and tell them you did.
5. **Post in French every single week.** It is the least contested ground you
   have and the reason this works.
