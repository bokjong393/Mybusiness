## Phase 2 — Audience monetization analysis

### The finding that overrides everything

The founder's LinkedIn is **55 followers, 150 impressions/week, 28 profile views in 90 days,
6 search appearances/week**. There is no audience. Any plan that routes through
content → followers → inbound leads produces zero revenue for approximately six months.

**Therefore: LinkedIn is used as a searchable database of buyers, not as a publishing platform.**
Precision outbound works at 55 followers. It works at 0 followers. Content runs in parallel as a
compounding asset and a credibility surface, not as the acquisition mechanism.

### Audience → Problem → Offer → Distribution → Revenue

| Segment | Has money? | Urgent problem? | Founder credible? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boutique consultancies & research firms (Africa-focused) | **Yes** | Lumpy research demand they can't staff | Yes — judged on samples, not degrees | **PRIMARY (recurring)** |
| Companies expanding across the EN/FR West Africa line | **Yes** | Deadline-bound documents and entry decisions | Yes — biography is the qualification | **PRIMARY (first cash)** |
| NGOs / development contractors in WAEMU | **Yes** | French submissions on donor deadlines | Yes | Secondary — slow procurement |
| VC/PE with West Africa mandates | Yes | Deal-support research | Partially — credential gap bites | Later, after portfolio |
| Diaspora property investors | Yes | Fraud exposure, no eyes on ground | Partially | Opportunistic; liability risk |
| Startup founders pre-funding | **Weak** | Decks, models | Yes | Attach product only |
| Students, job seekers, peers | **No** | Real pain, no budget | Yes | **Do not build a business here** |

### Where recurring revenue actually lives

Not in the first sale. Recurring revenue appears when a buyer has a **continuous** flow of the
work — a consultancy with a monthly research load, a firm publishing bilingual material every
month. That is why the ladder ends at retainers, not at bigger one-off projects.

### Problems that need software vs. services

Everything in the first 90 days is a **service delivered manually in Google Docs**. Software is
justified only at the point where the same research is being sold repeatedly to different buyers
(the WAEMU regulatory tracker, M7). That is a Year-1 decision, not a Month-1 one.

## Phase 4 — Selection

### 1. Fastest cash-flow opportunity → **M1: EN⇄FR business & financial document localization**

Highest weighted score (7.55). Days-to-weeks to a first sale, ~95% margin, no credential gate
(nobody asks a translator where they went to university), and the founder's Executive-VA
document skill is directly on-point. Its weakness — low defensibility — does not matter for a
first revenue line whose real job is to produce a paid client, a testimonial, and market contact.

### 2. Best long-term scalable business → **M3: Research-as-a-Service retainers → M7 data product**

Highest recurring (9) and scalability (8) scores among executable models. Retainers convert
founder time into predictable monthly revenue and are the only structure that reaches
$3,000/month without a punishing project treadmill. The research produced under retainer
becomes the raw material for a WAEMU regulatory/company dataset — the eventual product that
scales beyond founder hours.

### 3. Best hybrid strategy → **the ladder**

```
M1 Localization        →  M2 Research briefs   →  M3 Retainers        →  M7 Data product
(cash + testimonial)      (margin + position)     (recurring revenue)    (scale beyond time)
Week 2–6                  Month 2–4                Month 3–9              Year 1+
$150–600/project          $600–2,500/brief         $500–1,500/month       Annual licence
```

**Why this wins:** every rung is sold to the *same buyer list*, so distribution work compounds
instead of restarting. A localization client who trusts you is the warmest possible buyer for a
research brief; a brief buyer who came back twice is the natural retainer conversation. Nothing is
speculative — each step is unlocked by revenue from the step before, and no step requires
technology the founder cannot afford.

### Why NOT the obvious alternatives

- **Not generic data analysis / Power BI** (M12, 4.50): the certificates point here, the economics
  do not. Global commodity competition, zero regional or language advantage.
- **Not a student community or job-seeker product** (M11/M13): the founder's natural audience is
  peers with no budget. Monetizing your own demographic is the most common way this fails.
- **Not a paid newsletter or SaaS yet** (M10/M7): both need an audience that does not exist and
  recurring card payments that are currently blocked.

## Hard constraints that shape everything

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Cannot currently receive money** (no rail has ever paid out; Stripe unavailable in Nigeria) | **Blocker #1.** Solve before outreach — a "yes" you cannot collect on is worse than a no. |
| **Never been paid by a client** | First paid job matters more than its price. Take a small one to break the seal. |
| **One work sample** | Second, public, bilingual sample is a Week-1 deliverable. Costs $0. |
| **<$100 budget** | Free tier only. If anything must be bought before a customer pays, we chose wrong. |
| **55 followers** | Outbound is the channel. Content is the asset, not the engine. |
| **Undergraduate credential vs. "Analyst" positioning** | Win on work samples and specificity. Never imply seniority you don't have — one check destroys the sale. |
| **Native EN/FR + Nigeria/Benin** | The moat. Lead with it in every message. |

## Revenue expectations — stated honestly

The founder's success bar is **$3,000/month**. That is achievable, and it is **not** achievable in
30 days from a standing start with no track record, no samples in market, and no way to get paid.
Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

| Milestone | Realistic timing | What it looks like |

| $500/month | Month 2 | 2 small projects |
