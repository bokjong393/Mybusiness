# CUSTOMER.md — Who We Sell To

**Status:** Hypothesis. Every claim below is unverified until we have had 10 real conversations. Update after each one.

---

## Ideal Customer Profile

| Attribute | Target |
|---|---|
| **Business type** | B2B lead generation, outbound, SDR-as-a-service, or demand-gen agency |
| **Size** | 2–15 people. Big enough to have clients, small enough that the founder still does delivery. |
| **Geography** | US, UK, Canada, Australia. English-first, USD/GBP budgets. |
| **Clients served** | 3–15 active retainer clients |
| **Their pricing** | $2,000–8,000/month per client |
| **Buying authority** | The founder or head of delivery. **One person, no committee.** This is the single most important attribute. |
| **Existing spend** | Already pays for Apollo/Clay/Instantly seats and often a VA |
| **Detectable signal** | Hiring an SDR or researcher · publicly complaining about data quality · posting about campaign performance · listed in an agency directory with a lead-gen specialty |

### Why this profile and not their end clients

Selling to the agency's *client* (the SaaS company, the consultancy) means competing on trust, sitting through procurement, and losing on being an unknown foreign student. Selling to the *agency* means competing on output quality alone, in a market where the buyer resells our work and therefore has a direct financial incentive to say yes quickly.

**One buyer, one decision, no procurement, recurring need, and a structural reason to prefer a cheap capable vendor.** That is the whole reason this ICP was chosen.

### Anti-ICP — do not sell to these

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Agencies over ~30 people | Procurement, vendor onboarding, security reviews. We lose on paperwork. |
| Solo freelancers | No budget, and they do the research themselves — they are our competition, not our customer. |
| Agencies with an in-house data team | The pain is already solved internally. |
| Anyone whose first question is "where are you based?" before seeing the work | They are buying a vendor profile, not an outcome. Wrong buyer, and unwinnable. |
| End clients (SaaS, consultancies) — *for now* | Longer cycles, higher trust bar. Revisit at month 6 with case studies. |

## What they do today

| Current solution | Cost | Why it disappoints |
|---|---|---|
| Offshore VA | $300–800/mo | Produces volume without judgment. Cannot tell a real signal from a stale one. |
| Apollo / Clay | $100–500/mo | Everyone has the same data. No verification, no recency. |
| Founder does it personally | Their most expensive hour | Does not scale. Directly caps the agency's growth. |
| Buy a list | $200–2,000 | Stale on arrival, unsourced, often burns the sending domain. |

## What would make them switch

In descending order of power:
1. **Seeing finished work before paying.** Removes all risk from the decision.
2. **A price low enough to approve alone.** Under ~$150 means no meeting, no partner discussion.
3. **Citations.** They can verify one row in ten seconds and trust the other forty-nine.
4. **Time back.** The buyer's real currency. Frame everything in hours, not rows.
5. **White-label.** They can resell it as their own capability at a markup.

## Objection map

| They say | They mean | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "We use Clay for this." | *Why pay a person?* | "Clay finds companies. It doesn't decide which signals are still true. That check is what I'm selling — and it's why the sample I sent has dates and sources on it." |
| "Where are you based?" | *Is this safe?* | Answer plainly and immediately, then redirect to the work. Never be evasive — evasion confirms the fear. "Nigeria. That's why $147 buys what a US freelancer charges $600 for. The sample I sent shows the quality; judge me on that." |
| "How do I pay you?" | *Is this going to be awkward?* | "Standard invoice, card or bank transfer, USD. Same as any US vendor." Have the rail set up **before** this question arrives. |
| "Send more samples." | *Testing whether you'll work for free.* | One more small sample, then a price. "Happy to — here are 3 more. If these land, the 50-row pack is $147 and I'll have it to you in 5 days." |
| "Too expensive." (at $147) | Almost never real; usually a fit or trust problem. | "What would it need to produce to be obviously worth it?" Then either fix the offer or disqualify. Do not discount. |
| "Not right now." | Timing, or a polite no. | "Understood. Want me to send 3 fresh signals in a month, no charge, and you can decide then?" Converts a no into a scheduled follow-up. |

## Where they gather

- LinkedIn — most active channel; agency founders post constantly
- Clutch.co, Sortlist, agency directories — full public lists of exactly our ICP
- r/agency, r/sales, r/leadgeneration
- Facebook and Slack groups for agency owners
- Upwork — agencies post research and lead-gen jobs; filter for "No Hires" clients
- Their own cold outreach — if they email us, they are our ICP and have just identified themselves

## Open questions to resolve in the first 10 conversations

1. What do they *currently* pay for research, in total, per month?
2. Which signal types actually produce replies for them? (This is the single most valuable thing we can learn — it defines the product.)
3. Do they want raw rows, or rows plus suggested opening lines?
4. What made them fire their last research vendor?
5. How many clients do they turn away because delivery capacity is short?
6. Do they care about white-labelling, or is it irrelevant to them?
