## The follow-up

Send **7 working days** after applying, if you have heard nothing. Reply to your original email so the
thread stays together.

> Subject: Re: Finance Intern — Peace Sossa
>
> Dear [[Name]],
>
> I applied for the Finance Intern role on [[date]] and wanted to check whether the position is still
> open. I remain very interested, and I am happy to send any further information that would help.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> Peace Sossa
> [[phone]]

Follow up **once**. If there is still no reply after another two weeks, mark it Ghosted and move on —
that is the employer's failure of manners, not a verdict on you.

## Volume, honestly

An internship search is a numbers game and the numbers are not flattering to anyone. Expect roughly
**1 interview per 10–15 well-targeted applications**, and treat 5 quality tailored applications a week
as a good week. Five tailored applications beat thirty copy-pasted ones — but one tailored application
a month beats nothing, so the real enemy is stopping.

Where to look, beyond aggregators like JobDockets:

- **Direct careers pages** of Nigerian banks, fintechs and FMCGs — GTCO, Zenith, Access, Moniepoint,
  Flutterwave, Paystack, Kuda, Interswitch, Dangote, Nestlé Nigeria, Unilever Nigeria
- **LinkedIn Jobs**, filtered to Internship + Nigeria + Remote, with alerts saved
- **MyJobMag, Jobberman, Jobgurus** — established Nigerian boards
- **Francophone-facing employers**: anything with operations in Benin, Togo, Côte d'Ivoire or Senegal.
  Your French is a genuine differentiator here and almost nowhere else. Search French-language listings
  too — "stagiaire finance", "stage finance Cotonou/Abidjan/Dakar" — including remote ones.
- **Your own network**: lecturers, Miva alumni groups, church, family friends. Referrals convert far
  better than cold applications, and most people never ask.
