## What the company actually does

**Metro Group is a real estate developer, not a fintech.** Publicly available information describes
property development, construction and delivery across Lagos and Abuja, and — the important part —
helping ordinary Nigerians into homeownership through **NHF (National Housing Fund) financing and
rent-to-own schemes**.

This matters more than it looks, because it explains the job description.

## Why the job description reads like a fintech role

The posting talks about "liquidity across multiple banks, wallets, currencies, and payment corridors",
"settlements", and working with "Product and Engineering". That sounds like a payments company. In a
**rent-to-own property business it is exactly the same problem**:

| The posting says | In a rent-to-own developer that means |
|---|---|
| Monitor liquidity across multiple banks, wallets, currencies | Hundreds of buyers paying monthly instalments into several collection accounts and wallets — plus diaspora buyers remitting in GBP, USD and EUR |
| Daily/weekly reports on revenue, margins, transaction volumes | Instalment collections: who paid, how much came in, how many are behind |
| Settlements, internal transfers, vendor payments | Paying contractors, suppliers and artisans on live construction sites |
| Reconciliation | Matching every incoming payment to the right buyer's account — the single hardest operational problem in rent-to-own |
| Audit and regulatory compliance | NHF financing carries FMBN reporting obligations |

**This is your edge.** Almost every other applicant will read "finance intern" and write a generic
letter. If you show you understand that their finance function is really an *instalment collections and
reconciliation* engine, you will stand out immediately. That is the hook in the cover letter.

> Caveat, stated honestly: this is inferred from public information plus the job description, not
> confirmed by the company. Read `mtscape.com` yourself before you write, and treat it as a strong
> working hypothesis — confident, not presumptuous.

## Do this before you apply — 2 weekends, and it changes the outcome

Right now your CV says you studied economics. After this it says you have done the job.

**Project 1 — Collections & Liquidity Tracker** *(the one that matters)*
Build it in Excel. Invent 40 rent-to-own buyers on monthly instalments paying into 3 bank accounts and
1 wallet, with a few paying in USD or GBP. Then build:
- a reconciliation sheet matching each receipt to the right buyer
- flags for short payments, late payers and unmatched receipts
- a weekly liquidity summary: opening balance, collections in, vendor payments out, closing position
  per account and per currency

That is line-for-line their job description. Bring it to the interview.

**Project 2 — Three-statement model.** 12 linked months (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow) for a small
property business. Proves the "financial modelling" requirement outright.

**Excel gaps to close:** XLOOKUP, SUMIFS, pivot tables, IFERROR, `$` absolute references. A focused
weekend genuinely covers the "basic proficiency" bar.

**Accounting software:** if you have used none, install **Wave** (free) and run one month of dummy
transactions. Then the CV line is true.

## Verify and settle these — before you accept anything

The company is real and CAC-registered, the email is on its own domain, and ₦200,000 is a genuine
market stipend rather than bait. Nothing here looks wrong. These are ordinary due-diligence items:

- [ ] **Is ₦200,000 monthly?** The posting does not say. Ask before accepting.
- [ ] **Where exactly is the office, and can you get there daily?** The listing says "location not
      specified (fully on-site)"; the head office is Oniru, Lagos. Confirm the site and think honestly
      about the commute — Oniru/VI traffic is punishing and the cost comes out of the ₦200,000.
- [ ] **How does "full time, on-site" fit around 200L?** Your course is fully online, which is what makes
      this even possible — but a full-time on-site role plus coursework is real strain. Raise your
      availability yourself rather than waiting to be caught out; being upfront reads as integrity, and
      integrity is literally on their requirements list.
- [ ] **Is it paid from month one, and is there a written offer?** Get the terms in writing.
- [ ] **The role's real content.** The JD's treasury language may be templated. Ask what the first month
      actually involves.

**Standing rules, for this and every application:** never pay a fee of any kind, never give your BVN to
apply, and never share bank details before a signed offer.
