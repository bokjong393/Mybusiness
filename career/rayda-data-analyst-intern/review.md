## 2. What the job actually is

Nine listed duties, grouped by what they really mean:

**The bulk of it — data plumbing.** Collect, clean and organise data from different business
systems. Validation and quality checks. Document datasets and processes. Improve availability
and usability of business data.

**The visible part — reporting.** Regular reports and dashboards for Product, Growth and
Commercial teams. Track and report key business and product metrics.

**The part that grows you — analysis.** Identify trends and areas needing attention. Translate
business questions into simple analyses. Ad hoc requests.

Two details worth catching:

- **"Support the Data Analyst"** — singular. There's an existing analyst, so you'd have someone
  to learn from rather than being the first data person guessing alone.
- **"Product, Growth, Commercial and other teams"** — you'd sit across the whole business. For
  someone who wants to run her own company, that vantage point is the most valuable thing here.

**Duty one is your current job.** *"Support the collection, cleaning and organisation of data
from different business systems"* is a description of what you already do with IMF, NIBSS, CBN,
EFInA and GSMA data. The systems are different; the work is identical.

## 3. The money and the hours

| | |
|---|---|
| **Salary** | ₦80,000 – ₦100,000 monthly |
| **Job type** | Full time |
| **Experience** | 0–1 year |
| **Location** | Remote |
| **Opened** | 21 August 2026 |

**On the money.** Nigeria's national minimum wage is ₦70,000/month, so this is roughly 1.1× to
1.4× minimum wage for a full-time technology role. That's low in absolute terms.

But you're in **Okpoma, Cross River — not Lagos**, and that changes the arithmetic more than
people admit. The same figure that would be difficult in Lekki is workable in Cross River,
and remote means no transport, no relocation, no city rent. I flagged this as a weak offer when
I assumed Lagos. For you specifically it is a materially better one.

The honest framing is still: you're buying the first data-analyst line on your CV, a named
analyst to learn from, and provable full-time remote work at a funded company. Treat it as a
twelve-month trade.

**One thing to note about the band.** They ask for 0–1 year of experience and you have 17
months. You're not scraping in at the bottom of this range — you're at the top of it. That is
worth knowing before any conversation about money.

**On the hours — this is still the real decision.** Full time alongside 200L Economics isn't a
formality. Miva being asynchronous is the only reason it's feasible.

- *"I can do 40 hours and still pass well."* → Apply, take it if offered.
- *"Maybe 20–25 hours."* → Still apply, and **ask about hours in the first conversation**, not
  after an offer. A 27-person startup flexes constantly. Asking is normal; asking late looks bad.
- *"My grades would suffer."* → Then don't. The degree is the asset you're two years into.

Ask them plainly: *"The posting lists this as full time — is there flexibility on hours for a
current student, or is 40 hours firm?"*
