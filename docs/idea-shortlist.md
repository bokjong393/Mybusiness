# The Shortlist — 9 AI Digital Product Ideas, Scored

Scored 1–5 on five axes, out of 25. The axes are weighted for *my* situation:
a full-time economics student with limited capital who needs cash soon but is
building something that outlives the hours I put in.

- **Cash speed** — how fast to the first payment
- **Capital** — how little money it takes to start (5 = nearly free)
- **Moat** — how hard it is for 10,000 other people to copy me
- **Ceiling** — how big it can get
- **Fit** — my time, skills, languages, credibility

---

## 1. Bilingual WhatsApp AI setup for small businesses — 21/25

`Cash speed 5 · Capital 5 · Moat 3 · Ceiling 3 · Fit 5`

**What:** Set up, configure and train small businesses on the AI assistant now built
into WhatsApp Business. Not writing code — writing the business's knowledge: FAQ,
product catalogue, tone, escalation rules, then teaching the owner to run it.

**Revised after a competitive check — see `competitive-reality.md`.** Meta Business
Agent went global on 3 June 2026 and sets up in minutes; over a million businesses
already use it. So "I'll configure your bot" is not a scarce skill, and the ₦75k–250k
setup fee below is squeezed between a free tool and ₦900k agency builds from registered
Nigerian firms.

**What survives:** not the setup — the *knowledge* the agent answers from. The FAQ,
catalogue, prices, policies and tone, written in two languages, for an owner who has
nothing written down. Worth ₦30,000–₦50,000, not ₦75,000–₦250,000.

**Why the demand is real:** 95% of Nigeria's digital population uses WhatsApp. Roughly
78% of Sub-Saharan SMEs already sell on it. Meta counts ~14M Nigerian SMEs on its
platforms. These are businesses losing sales at 11pm because nobody replies.

**Price (revised down):** ₦30,000–₦50,000 for the knowledge build, then
₦20,000–₦40,000/month to maintain it. In XOF: 20,000–35,000, then 15,000–25,000/month.

**First move:** Build one on a fake boutique. Screen-record it answering a customer at
midnight. That 40-second video is the entire sales pitch.

**Honest weakness:** It trades my hours for money. That is exactly why it is the
*funding* move and not the destination.

## 2. "L'IA pour ton business" — French AI course for francophone African entrepreneurs — 21/25

`Cash speed 2 · Capital 4 · Moat 5 · Ceiling 5 · Fit 5`

**What:** A French-language course and toolkit teaching small business owners in Benin,
Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Cameroon and DRC to actually use ChatGPT/Claude for customer
replies, marketing copy, pricing, and bookkeeping. Not "what is AI" — a sequence of
copy-paste workflows for a specific kind of business.

**Why it is the best idea here:** Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Cameroon and DRC together are
250M+ people underpenetrated by English-language platforms. Reports specifically
identify French-language curriculum gaps as the thing limiting e-learning growth in the
region. Demand for digital skills training across Africa is up 56%. And business and
entrepreneurship courses — "how to start an import business", "digital marketing for
small businesses" — are documented as the top performers on the continent.

Meanwhile essentially all good AI education is in English.

**Why I can win it:** I am Beninese-Nigerian. I speak both languages natively, and I
understand both markets from the inside. Someone can copy my curriculum in a week. They
cannot copy that. This is the only idea on this list where my unfair advantage is
structural rather than a head start.

**Price ladder:** 5,000 XOF (~$8) starter guide → 25,000 XOF (~$40) core course →
75,000 XOF (~$125) live cohort. Sell the same thing in NGN to the Nigerian market later.

**First move:** A free French lead magnet — *"10 prompts ChatGPT pour vendre plus sur
WhatsApp"* — to start collecting WhatsApp numbers before anything is built.

## 3. English→French localization on revenue share — 21/25

`Cash speed 3 · Capital 5 · Moat 4 · Ceiling 4 · Fit 5`

**What:** Find Nigerian creators with courses already *proven* to sell in English.
Offer to translate, culturally adapt, and run francophone distribution for 30–50% of
francophone revenue. They keep 100% of their existing market and gain a new one at zero
cost. I build no product and carry no demand risk.

**Why it is underrated:** The single biggest risk in digital products is making
something nobody wants. This idea deletes that risk entirely — the product has already
proven demand. My only job is the part I am uniquely good at.

**Where to find them:** Top sellers on Selar. Marketing, business, and import/export
creators are the consistent top earners.

**Price:** 30% rev share if they run promotion, 50% if I run francophone distribution
myself. Never charge a translation fee — take the upside.

**First move:** One page in French showing a sample module translated and adapted, sent
cold to 10 creators. Do the first one free to get the case study.

## 4. Francophone business content channel — 19/25

`Cash speed 1 · Capital 5 · Moat 4 · Ceiling 5 · Fit 4`

**What:** Short-form French video on AI + small business + money, aimed at Cotonou,
Abidjan, Dakar, Douala. Not a product — the distribution asset that makes idea #2 work.

**Why it belongs on the list anyway:** Building is solved; distribution is now the hard
part of every digital business. The ₦45,000-per-creator average exists because most
creators build a product and *then* look for buyers. This is the reverse order.

**Do not** run it for ad revenue — RPM in African geographies is too low to matter. It
is a funnel, and its only KPI is WhatsApp numbers collected.

**Runs in parallel with #2, never instead of it.**

## 5. Bilingual AI content retainer for African brands — 18/25

`Cash speed 4 · Capital 5 · Moat 2 · Ceiling 3 · Fit 4`

**What:** A monthly retainer producing 30 days of social content in *both* English and
French, AI-drafted and human-edited by me.

**Why the bilingual framing matters:** Plain AI content writing is brutally
competitive and price-collapsing. "One retainer, two languages, two markets" is a
different product that very few freelancers can offer.

**Price:** ₦60,000–₦150,000/month per client. Five clients ≈ ₦300,000–₦750,000/month.

**Weakness:** Low moat, and it is still hours-for-money. Good bridge income, weak
destination.

## 6. A niche AI operating system (not a prompt pack) — 18/25

`Cash speed 3 · Capital 5 · Moat 3 · Ceiling 3 · Fit 4`

**What:** One tightly-scoped business, fully systematised: prompts + workflow
documentation + tracking template + usage guide, sold as a single product. Candidate
niche: **the import business** (sourcing from China, landing and selling in
Nigeria/Benin), which is repeatedly identified as a top-performing topic in Africa.

**The pricing evidence:** Bundled systems at $27–$97 out-earn single prompt packs by
roughly 8x in total revenue despite selling fewer units. The $2.99 prompt pack market
is a race to zero; the $49 system market is not.

**Price:** ₦25,000–₦45,000 (~$29–$69).

## 7. AI-assisted scholarship and grant applications (EN + FR) — 18/25

`Cash speed 3 · Capital 5 · Moat 3 · Ceiling 3 · Fit 4`

**What:** Coaching plus AI-assisted drafting for African students applying to
scholarships and grants — including the francophone programmes almost nobody covers in
English.

**Real pain, real budget, bilingual advantage.**

**Critical rule:** Price it as document coaching. Never guarantee an outcome, and never
write an application as the applicant. Charge ₦15,000–₦40,000 per application package.

## 8. AI study systems for Nigerian open-university students — 17/25

`Cash speed 4 · Capital 5 · Moat 2 · Ceiling 2 · Fit 4`

**What:** Course-specific study systems for Miva/NOUN students — structured summaries,
AI-generated practice questions with worked explanations, revision schedules.

**Why it is credible:** I am a 200L student living this exact problem.

**Why it scores low:** The Nigerian past-question market is crowded and anchored at
₦1,000–₦3,000, which caps the ceiling hard. And the integrity line must stay bright:
this is a study aid, never ghostwritten coursework. Cross that line and the business is
one screenshot away from dead.

## 9. Micro-SaaS / AI tool — 13/25

`Cash speed 1 · Capital 2 · Moat 3 · Ceiling 5 · Fit 2`

**Not now — and here is the honest math.** Thin AI wrappers run 25–35% gross margins
against 70–85% for real SaaS, because the model bill scales with usage. Around 70% of
micro-SaaS products never clear $1,000 MRR, and the median *profitable* one sits near
$4,200 MRR. Roughly 90% of thin wrappers are projected to fail by end of 2026.

The ones that work are vertical and moated by data, community, or workflow — which
means they are built *by* someone who already owns an audience in that vertical.

**Revisit at month 9+,** if idea #2 has given me a francophone audience. Then the tool
has somewhere to launch into. Building it first is building a shop in the desert.

## What I deliberately rejected

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Generic ChatGPT prompt packs | The $2.99 tier is saturated and competing on identical search terms. Margins have collapsed. |
| Generic Notion / productivity templates | Same problem — intense competition, compressed margins, no defensibility. |
| Faceless AI YouTube for ad revenue | Ad RPM in African geos is too low to build on. Only worth it as a funnel. |
| "AI automation agency" as a general label | The field went from ~2,000 agencies in 2024 to 12,000+ in 2026. Generic positioning is now invisible; only a named vertical and a named outcome sells. |
| Dropshipping-style "AI business in a box" resale | Resold PLR products have no moat and burn audience trust permanently. |
| Korean-language products | I am a beginner. No credibility yet. Revisit in years, not months. |
