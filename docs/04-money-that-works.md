# 04 — Money That Works For You

> *"Make my money work for me."*

This is the actual goal. Everything in documents 01–03 exists to feed this one. It is
also the part almost nobody executes, because earning feels like progress and saving
feels like nothing is happening.

A note before the numbers: you're studying economics, so treat this document as the
applied version of your coursework. Real yield curves, real inflation, real currency
risk — with your own money on the line, which is a far better teacher than any
textbook.

---

## The problem specific to earning in Nigeria

Two forces work against you at once:

1. **Naira depreciation.** Money held in naira loses purchasing power against imports,
   software, travel and dollar-priced anything.
2. **Inflation.** Even naira returns need to clear inflation before you've gained
   anything real.

So the goal isn't "save money." It's **hold assets that grow faster than your currency
loses value** — and hold *some* of them in a currency that isn't yours.

---

## The order of operations

Do these in sequence. Skipping ahead is how people end up with an investment portfolio
and no emergency fund, which is a fast route to selling assets at the worst possible
moment.

### Step 0 — Separate the money *(do this immediately)*

Three buckets, three accounts:

| Bucket | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Business** | Client money lands here. Not spending money. |
| **Tax** | 20–25% of net income, moved the day payment arrives. |
| **Personal** | What you actually pay yourself. |

Paying yourself a fixed amount and leaving the rest in the business is the single
habit that separates people who build businesses from people who have busy months.

### Step 1 — Emergency fund: 3 months of expenses

Boring, unglamorous, non-negotiable. Freelance income is lumpy; a client vanishes,
a platform deactivates an account, a laptop dies. Without a buffer, one bad month
forces you to take terrible work at terrible rates — and that undoes months of rate
progress.

**Where:** a Nigerian money-market fund. Yields above **20%** in 2026, next-day
liquidity, minimums as low as **₦5,000–₦10,000**. Top performers include Coronation
Money Market Fund (**23.74%** one-year) and ARM Money Market Fund (**21.97%**)
([NairaCompare](https://nairacompare.ng/blogs/best-performing-money-market-funds-in-nigeria)).
Accessible via **Cowrywise**, **PiggyVest**, **Risevest**, or directly with the fund
managers.

Money-market funds are the right home for this because you can get the money out
quickly. That is the entire point of an emergency fund.

### Step 2 — Currency hedge: get some savings into USD

Once the emergency fund is full, start converting a fixed share of income — **30–50%
is a reasonable target** — into dollar-denominated assets.

| Option | Returns (USD terms) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **USD money-market / Eurobond funds** | ~5–8%/yr | ARM Discovery Dollar ~7.83%, FBNQuest Eurobond ~8.24% — but minimums are $1,000–$2,000 |
| **Risevest / Cowrywise dollar plans** | ~5–8%/yr | Minimums from **$10 / ₦5,000** — the realistic starting point for you |
| **Simply holding USD** | 0% | Beats naira depreciation, earns nothing. Fine for short-term reserves only. |

The mathematics that matter here: a naira fund yielding 22% and a dollar fund yielding
7% are not straightforwardly comparable. If the naira depreciates more than ~15%
against the dollar over the year, the dollar fund wins. Hold both, and stop trying to
predict which way it goes — you can't, and neither can anyone selling you a forecast.

### Step 3 — Longer-term naira instruments

Once steps 1 and 2 are solid:

- **Treasury bills:** **16–20%** depending on tenor, backed by the federal government.
  Available through banks (₦50,000+ minimums, 0.125–0.25% fees) or via apps like
  Cowrywise, PiggyVest and Risevest from **₦10,000**
  ([NairaCompare](https://nairacompare.ng/blogs/nigerias-h2-2026-investment-outlook-where-to-put-your-money)).
- **Fixed-income funds:** higher yields than money-market funds — Coronation Fixed
  Income around **26.82%**, ARM Fixed Income **24.56%** — with 2–7 day redemption
  periods. More return, less liquidity.

### Step 4 — Global equity exposure

Long horizon, higher volatility, the highest historical real returns. At 21, your
time horizon is the single greatest financial advantage you will ever have — you can
absorb volatility that a 55-year-old cannot.

Accessible from Nigeria via Risevest, Bamboo, Trove, Chaka and similar. Global index
funds over individual stocks: you are buying decades of compounding, not trying to
pick winners.

**Only after steps 1–3.** Equities can fall 30% and stay there for two years. That's
survivable with an emergency fund and unsurvivable without one.

### Step 5 — Reinvest in the business

Frequently the highest-return option available and the one people forget is an
investment at all.

₦150,000 spent on a course that raises your rate from $15 to $30/hour returns more
than any fund on this page — 4 hours a week at that new rate repays it in a month,
and then keeps paying forever. The same applies to hiring your first contractor to
handle delivery so you can sell instead.

**The test:** does this spending increase what I can charge, or how much I can deliver
without me? If neither, it's a cost, not an investment.

## The allocation, once income is stable

A reasonable starting frame for someone in your position — adjust to your own risk
tolerance, don't treat it as gospel:

| Allocation | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **30%** | Naira money-market fund | Liquidity + 20%+ yield |
| **30%** | USD-denominated assets | Currency hedge |
| **20%** | Global equities | Long-run growth |
| **20%** | Reinvested in the business | Usually the highest return of all |

Rebalance twice a year. Don't check it daily — daily checking produces anxiety and
bad decisions, in that order.

## The thing nobody says about "passive income"

There is no such thing as income that requires nothing.

What exists is income **decoupled from your hours** — which is different, and better,
and still requires work. A digital product needs updating and marketing. A fund
portfolio needs contributions and periodic rebalancing. A business that runs without
you needs people, and people need managing.

The realistic version of the dream is this: **by your late twenties, a business that
runs on systems and staff rather than your presence, plus a portfolio that grows
whether you work that month or not.** That is entirely achievable from where you're
standing. It's also roughly a seven-year project, and the people who get there are
the ones who accepted that timeline instead of chasing a shorter one.

Which is exactly what makes the "₦100,000 a day, start today" messages so effective —
they're selling the timeline, not the outcome. You already know the outcome is real.
That's why the fake shortcut is tempting.

## The compounding you actually have

You are 21. If you invest **₦50,000 a month** from age 22 to 32 at a **20% nominal
return**, you end with roughly **₦19 million** — of which about ₦13 million is growth,
not contribution. Extend that to 40 and the number becomes absurd.

That is not a promise. Returns vary, inflation eats part of it, and Nigerian yields
above 20% reflect real risk and real inflation. But the *shape* is right: at your age,
consistency dominates size. A ₦50,000/month habit starting now beats a ₦500,000/month
habit starting at 35, and it isn't close.

The most valuable asset in this entire repository is your age, and it is the only one
that depletes whether or not you use it.
