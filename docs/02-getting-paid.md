# 02 — Getting Paid: Rails, Tax, Contracts, and Scams

Earning the money is half the job. Keeping it — legally, in a currency that holds its
value, without being robbed — is the other half. This is the least exciting document
here and the one that will save you the most money.

---

## 1. Payment rails: how dollars actually reach you

You need a **virtual USD account** — US (and ideally GBP/EUR) account details in your
name that international clients and platforms can pay into, which you then withdraw to
your Nigerian bank in naira or hold in dollars.

| Tool | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Payoneer** | Marketplace payouts — Upwork, Fiverr, Toptal, Amazon | The default for platform work. Widely accepted, established. |
| **Grey** | Direct client payments | Built specifically for freelancers in countries that struggled to receive money. Virtual USD/GBP/EUR accounts. |
| **Raenest** (formerly Geegpay) | Direct client payments | Nigerian-built, freelancer-focused, competitive fees. |
| **Wise** | Lowest conversion cost | Generally the cheapest rates and most transparent fees, but Nigerian account-opening rules shift — verify current status. |
| **Cleva / Hurupay** | Newer USD alternatives | Growing quickly, worth comparing on fees. |

**The setup experienced Nigerian freelancers actually use** is not one tool — it's
two: **Payoneer for marketplace payouts, Grey or Raenest for direct client payments.**
They're complements, not competitors
([Nathan Ojaokomo](https://nathanojaokomo.com/blog/payoneer-alternatives),
[Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/best-platforms-for-receiving-international-payments-africa-2026/)).

For clients paying **in naira** (Nigerian tutoring students, local businesses, digital
product buyers): **Paystack** or **Flutterwave** for payment links and invoices,
**Selar** if you're selling digital products.

### Practical rules

1. **Open the account before you need it.** Verification takes days. Do not make a
   client wait while you scramble.
2. **Do not run a business through your personal bank account.** Separate accounts,
   from day one — for tax, for sanity, and for knowing whether you're actually
   profitable.
3. **Watch the fees.** 2% here, a bad FX spread there — on $500/month that's real
   money. Compare the *effective* rate you receive, not the advertised rate.
4. **Keep a share in dollars.** Do not convert everything to naira on arrival.
   See [04 — Money That Works](04-money-that-works.md).

## 2. Tax: this changed on 1 January 2026

Nigeria's **Nigeria Tax Act 2025** took effect on **1 January 2026**, and it brought
freelancers, remote workers and content creators squarely into scope
([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/06/nigeria-2026-tax-freelancers-influencers/)).

**What you need to know:**

- **First ₦800,000 per year (after allowable deductions) is exempt.** Below that
  threshold you owe no personal income tax.
- Above it, **progressive bands apply, capped at 25%.** Reporting on the new law
  indicates freelancers and remote workers pay **up to 23% on foreign earnings**
  ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/29/remote-workers-nigeria-income-tax-2026/)).
- **You self-declare.** Nobody deducts it for you. Self-assessment returns are due by
  **31 March** of the year following the income year.
- **Penalties are real:** from ₦50,000 for minor offences up to ₦1 million or
  imprisonment for serious violations.

**What to do about it, starting now:**

1. **Track every naira and dollar in.** A single spreadsheet is enough at your scale:
   date, client, amount, currency, platform, converted naira value.
2. **Track expenses too** — data, laptop, software subscriptions, platform fees,
   training. Legitimate business expenses reduce taxable income. Untracked expenses
   are money donated to the government for no reason.
3. **Set aside 20–25% of net income** in a separate account from your first payment.
   Not when you cross ₦800k — from the *first* payment. The habit is the point.
4. **Get a TIN.** You will need it eventually; getting it while small is painless.
5. **Once you're consistently over ₦800k/year, talk to an actual Nigerian tax
   professional.** One consultation costs less than one penalty.

> Nothing here is professional tax advice — it's a summary of public reporting on a
> law that is new and still settling. Verify with a qualified adviser before filing.

## 3. Contracts and getting paid on time

You will be tempted to skip this. Don't. Non-payment is the most common way new
freelancers lose money, and it is almost entirely preventable.

**The minimum viable protection:**

- **50% upfront for any new client, always.** Non-negotiable. A client who won't pay
  half in advance is telling you something true about themselves.
- **Write the scope down.** What you deliver, how many revision rounds, by what date,
  for how much. Email counts as a contract. "We agreed on WhatsApp" also counts, but
  only if you can find it.
- **Milestone payments on anything above $500.** Never carry more than 2 weeks of
  unpaid work for one client.
- **Use platform escrow when you're on a platform.** Upwork's fee buys you payment
  protection. A client who asks you to move off-platform to "avoid fees" is asking
  you to give up your only leverage — this is one of the oldest scams on the internet.
- **Kill fee for cancelled projects.** One line: *"If the project is cancelled after
  work begins, the deposit is non-refundable."*
- **Price in USD, invoice in USD.** Naira quotes on a 6-week project can lose real
  value before the invoice clears.

## 4. Crypto: legal, regulated, and not an income strategy

Since the **Investments and Securities Act 2025**, digital assets are formally
recognised as securities in Nigeria under SEC oversight. Crypto is **legal**, but
exchanges and service providers must be **SEC-registered**, and the SEC maintains an
"irregular operator alert" naming unlicensed platforms
([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/blockchain-crypto-assets-2026/nigeria/trends-and-developments),
[Lightspark](https://www.lightspark.com/knowledge/is-crypto-legal-in-nigeria)).
P2P trading isn't banned outright, but regulators are pushing volume onto licensed
platforms and banks scrutinise P2P flows.

**Where this fits in your plan:** as a *payment rail* it's occasionally useful. As an
*income strategy* it is speculation, not business — and "crypto trading" is the
wrapper on most of the schemes in the next section. If you touch it at all, use
SEC-licensed platforms only, and never with money you need.

## 5. The scam patterns hunting you in 2026

Nigerians have lost over **₦316 billion** to documented Ponzi schemes. The **CBEX**
collapse alone cost investors an estimated **$1 billion** in 2025
([Africa Check](https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/blog/guide-scam-trends-watch-out-2026-and-how-stay-safe-online)).
These are not stupid people. They are ordinary people shown a very good story.

### The five patterns

**1. The unsolicited "recruiter" DM.**
The single biggest online job scam in Nigeria in 2026: a WhatsApp or Instagram message
from a recruiter you never contacted, offering ₦30,000–₦100,000/day for data entry,
"liking posts," or "completing tasks." You complete a few small tasks, get paid a
little — then you're asked to deposit **₦15,000–₦25,000 to "unlock" higher earnings.**
It escalates into hundreds of thousands.
🚩 *You never applied. There's a deposit. The money is too good.*

**2. Fake investment platforms.**
Nigeria's SEC issued a public warning in May 2026 about unregistered platforms pushed
hard on WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, TikTok and Facebook.
🚩 *Guaranteed returns. "Double your money." Daily profits. Referral bonuses.*
Guaranteed returns do not exist. Anything paying you to recruit is paying you with
the recruits' money.

**3. AI deepfake endorsements.**
Realistic videos of respected public figures and celebrities "recommending" an
investment platform. In 2026 a video of a famous person saying something is not
evidence that they said it.

**4. Upfront-fee "jobs."**
Training fees, registration fees, equipment deposits, "certification." **Legitimate
employers do not charge you to work.** Ever. There is no exception to this rule.

**5. Course and mentorship funnels.**
Not always illegal, usually worthless: ₦50,000 to learn "forex secrets" or
"affiliate mastery" from someone whose only demonstrable income is selling that
course. Ask one question: *can they show income from the thing itself, not from
teaching it?*

### Your filter — four questions

1. **Did I go to them, or did they come to me?** Inbound offers are 10x more likely
   to be fraudulent.
2. **Am I being asked for money to earn money?** If yes, stop. Full stop.
3. **Is the return guaranteed?** Then it's a lie. Real returns are uncertain by
   definition.
4. **Does it pay me to recruit others?** Then I am the product, and the people I
   recruit are the payout.

### Verify before you commit

- Check the platform against the **SEC Nigeria** register and its irregular-operator
  alerts before putting money anywhere.
- Search `"[company name]" scam` and `"[company name]" review` — do it in French too;
  West African scam networks often run the same operation across both languages.
- Real employers hire through job boards, LinkedIn, or their own careers page — never
  exclusively through WhatsApp.
- Real remote employers pay through traceable rails, not gift cards or crypto.

> **The underlying rule.** Everything legitimate in this repository takes months and
> pays ordinary amounts of money. Anything promising to beat that timeline is selling
> you the story, and you are the revenue.
