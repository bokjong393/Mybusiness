# Competitive Reality Check

*Added after a fair challenge: "do you really think this is unique? It must exist already."*

It does exist already. The earlier claim in this repo — that nobody serves French-language
donor reporting for francophone African NGOs — was wrong. Here is what actually exists.

## Who is already in this market

| Product | Who built it | Where it operates | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SmartEval** | WEBGRAM, a software company based in **Dakar, Senegal** | Deployed in 18 African countries including **Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Benin, Mali, Cameroon** | Francophone-native, automated donor report generation. This is precisely the market and the feature set claimed as an open gap. |
| **DELTA Suivi-Évaluation** | — | Multiple African countries | French-language M&E, automatic report and dashboard generation |
| **SUCCESS** | — | 2,000+ installations across 25+ African countries | Financial and project management for development projects |
| **ActivityInfo** | — | Includes **Mali and DRC** | Humanitarian data management in francophone contexts |
| **LogAlto / TolaData / DevResults** | International | Global, incl. African NGOs | Logframes, indicator tracking, donor reporting |
| **Grantable / Instrumentl / Granted AI** | US | US nonprofits | AI grant *writing* and discovery — English, US funders |

A Senegalese company is already selling automated francophone donor reporting into Benin
and Côte d'Ivoire. Not a distant American incumbent — a regional one, with a team.

## What this actually means

**The software version of this idea is closed to me.** I cannot out-build WEBGRAM, DELTA
or LogAlto as one student with no capital and no engineering team. Any plan that ends in
"and then I build a francophone M&E platform" should be discarded now rather than in
eighteen months.

**But existing competition is evidence of a paying market, not a reason to leave.** A
niche with zero competitors is usually a niche with no money in it. Eight products
serving francophone African NGO reporting means organisations in Benin, Senegal and
Côte d'Ivoire *do* pay for this problem to go away.

## Where the actual opening is — and why it is smaller than claimed

Every product above sells to organisations with a budget line, a procurement process, and
someone to administer the system. That leaves two groups unserved, and it leaves them
unserved *for a reason*:

- **Freelance M&E consultants** who write reports for several NGOs and carry their own P&L
- **Three-person local NGOs** that will never procure a platform

They are unserved because they are poor and fragmented — which is exactly why no software
company wants them. That is an honest limitation, not a hidden treasure.

**What is genuinely still thin** is the narrative layer: turning field notes, photos and
numbers into the *prose* a specific donor requires, matched to that donor's template —
AFD, EU and UN formats all differ. The platforms above manage indicators and dashboards
well. They do not write your quarterly narrative for you in French.

## The revised conclusion

This becomes a **service**, not a product company.

Selling a service does not require beating SmartEval on features. It requires being the
person who *did the work* — fast, in French, at a price a consultant will pay out of their
own fee. Services compete on "it's done," not on a feature matrix.

| | Product company | Service business |
|---|---|---|
| Capital needed | High | Near zero |
| Time to first revenue | 6–18 months | Weeks |
| Competes with | WEBGRAM, LogAlto, DevResults | Other freelancers |
| Feasible for me now | No | Yes |

And this is exactly what the "manually deliver to 5–10 customers before building anything"
advice already said. The service phase is not a lesser version of the product — it is how
I find out whether a product-shaped gap exists at all. If after ten clients I am doing the
identical thing every time, that repeated thing is the product. If I am not, I still have
a business.

## The lesson worth keeping

**"Does it already exist?" is almost always yes, and is the wrong question.**

The better questions:

1. Is anyone serving the *specific* customer I can actually reach?
2. Can I reach them cheaply, given who I am and who I know?
3. Do they have money?

Uniqueness is not the asset. Access is.
