# The Recommended Stack

Three ideas ran within one point of each other at the top. That is not indecision —
they are three different *roles*, and they compound. Doing them in this order is the
actual recommendation.

## Why not just pick the highest scorer?

Because the top three fail alone and work together:

- **The WhatsApp service (#1)** makes money in weeks but never stops needing my hours.
- **The French course (#2)** scales without my hours but earns nothing for months, and
  I would be guessing at what to teach.
- **Localization rev-share (#3)** is pure leverage but needs credibility I don't have yet.

Run in sequence, each one solves the next one's problem.

---

## Move 1 — Cash engine (weeks 1–6)

**Bilingual WhatsApp AI setup. Target: 3 paying clients, ~₦300,000.**

The money matters least here. What I am actually buying:

1. **Proof** — filmed testimonials from real business owners
2. **Language** — the exact words owners use to describe their problem, which becomes
   my sales copy
3. **Curriculum** — every question a client asks me is a module in the course

Keep a running file called `client-questions.md` from day one. It is the single most
valuable output of this phase.

## Move 2 — Asset engine (weeks 4–14)

**`L'IA pour ton business`, built from that questions file.**

Overlap deliberately with Move 1. Ship in this order, smallest first:

| Step | Product | Price | Why |
|------|---------|-------|-----|
| 1 | Free French lead magnet | 0 | Collects WhatsApp numbers. The list is the asset. |
| 2 | Starter guide | 5,000 XOF (~$8) | Converts a follower into a *buyer* — the hardest step in the funnel |
| 3 | Core course | 25,000 XOF (~$40) | The real product |
| 4 | Live cohort | 75,000 XOF (~$125) | High margin, and forces the course to stay current |

Do not skip step 2. The distance from ₦0 to ₦1 is far longer than from ₦1 to ₦40,000 —
someone who has paid once is a fundamentally different person to sell to.

## Move 3 — Distribution, running continuously from week 6

**French short-form content.** One KPI only: WhatsApp numbers collected. Not views.

This is the part almost everyone skips, and it is the reason the average Selar creator
earns ₦45,000 a year. They built a product and *then* went looking for buyers.

## Move 3b — The high-rate service to test from week 8

**French donor-report writing for francophone NGO consultants.**

Freelance M&E consultants in Benin, Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire write quarterly narrative
reports to AFD, EU and UN templates. It is slow, repetitive, in French, and they are paid
per report out of their own fee — so they decide in a week, unlike an NGO whose
procurement takes months.

**Why a service and not software:** francophone M&E platforms already exist and are
regionally entrenched — WEBGRAM's SmartEval alone runs in 18 countries including Benin and
Côte d'Ivoire. I cannot out-build them. But none of them writes your narrative for you,
and a consultant paying for a finished report does not care about a feature matrix.
Full detail in `competitive-reality.md`.

**Headwind to respect:** France cut aid by 37% (€2.1bn) and Sahel appeals ran badly
underfunded. The sector is contracting — which is exactly why the work shifts from
salaried M&E staff to freelancers. That is the wedge.

**Test it cheaply:** three consultants, one report each, priced per report. If they come
back, it is real. If they don't, I have lost two weeks, not two quarters.

## Move 4 — Leverage (opportunistic, month 4+)

**Localization rev-share**, once I have one francophone launch to point at. Pitch 10
proven Selar creators. First one free for the case study, then 30–50%.

## What the money could look like

Not a promise — an arithmetic sketch of what the shape looks like if it works.

| Month | WhatsApp service | Products | Rough total |
|-------|------------------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | ₦0 (free pilots) | ₦0 | ₦0 |
| 2 | ₦150,000 | ₦0 | ₦150,000 |
| 3 | ₦200,000 + ₦50,000 retainer | ₦0 | ₦250,000 |
| 4 | ₦250,000 | ₦40,000 (starter guide) | ₦290,000 |
| 6 | ₦300,000 | ₦200,000 (course launch) | ₦500,000 |

The benchmark to keep in mind: a ₦3,000 product selling 100 copies a month is
₦300,000/month. That is the achievable shape — and it requires 100 buyers a month,
which is why Move 3 is not optional.

**The most likely failure mode** is not that a product flops. It is spending three
months building the course, launching to nobody, and concluding digital products don't
work. Move 1 and Move 3 exist specifically to prevent that.

## Rules I am setting for myself now, while it is easy

1. **Never sell hours I haven't got.** Cap the service at 4 active clients during term.
2. **Keep records from day one.** Nigeria is actively moving to tax creator-economy
   income — a spreadsheet of every naira in and out, starting now, costs nothing today
   and saves a great deal later.
3. **One offer, one market, until it works.** Adding a second product to fix a
   distribution problem is the classic trap.
4. **Free work only in exchange for a filmed testimonial**, agreed in writing before I start.
