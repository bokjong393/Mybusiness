# 03 — The First 90 Days

Written for a full-time 200-level student with roughly **15 hours a week** and
**near-zero startup capital**. Adjust the calendar, not the sequence.

**The strategy in one sentence:** stack a fast cash path on top of a slow moat path,
so money arrives while the valuable thing is being built.

- **Cash path (weeks 1–4):** AI training work or tutoring — money in weeks, low
  barrier, no portfolio required.
- **Moat path (weeks 1–12):** bilingual services for African fintech/SaaS — slower to
  start, far harder for anyone to take from you.

Do not add a third path. The failure mode is not laziness; it is having six
half-finished things.

---

## Weeks 1–2 — Foundations

**Goal: infrastructure exists, and one application is submitted.**

| # | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open a **Payoneer** account. Complete verification. | 1 hr + wait |
| 2 | Open a **Grey** or **Raenest** account for direct clients. | 1 hr |
| 3 | Open a **separate bank account** for business money only. | 1 hr |
| 4 | Start `income-tracker.csv` — date, client, amount, currency, fees, naira value. | 30 min |
| 5 | Write a **one-page profile** in English and French: who you are, what you do, who you do it for. | 3 hrs |
| 6 | Apply to **two** AI training platforms (Outlier, DataAnnotation, Mercor, Handshake AI). | 3 hrs |
| 7 | Create a Preply **or** italki tutor profile — French for English speakers. | 2 hrs |

**Your positioning line.** Write it now, use it everywhere:

> *"I help African fintech and SaaS companies reach francophone West Africa —
> localisation, support content and market research in publishable French. Economics
> student, Beninese-Nigerian, native-level in both languages."*

Note what that sentence does: it doesn't say "I'm a freelancer." It names a specific
customer and a specific problem. That difference is worth thousands of dollars a year.

**Expected earnings: ₦0.** This is correct and expected. Do not panic in week two.

---

## Weeks 3–4 — First proof

**Goal: three portfolio pieces and a first application to real work.**

You have no clients, so you build proof without them:

1. **Pick three real companies** expanding into francophone Africa — Moniepoint,
   Flutterwave, Paystack, Kuda, Yellow Card, Wave, or any SaaS with a French-language
   gap.
2. **Localise a real page of theirs into French** — a pricing page, an onboarding
   email, three help-centre articles. Do it properly, as if paid.
3. **Write a one-page teardown** for each: what's wrong with their current French
   presence, what you'd fix, why it costs them signups.

That is a portfolio *and* three warm outreach emails, produced by the same work. Send
each teardown to the company's growth or marketing lead. Expect no reply from most.
One reply is all you need.

Meanwhile: complete AI-training onboarding, take your first tutoring bookings, and
set your rate at the **low end** deliberately. You are buying reviews, not income.

**Expected earnings: $0–$80.**

---

## Weeks 5–8 — First real money

**Goal: money from at least two sources, and a rate increase.**

- Keep tutoring — target **3–5 sessions/week**. Ask every satisfied student for a
  written review. Reviews are the currency of every platform.
- Apply to **8–12 bilingual roles or gigs per week.** Upwork, Jobgether,
  Remote4Africa, LinkedIn, and direct outreach. Quantity matters at this stage; you
  are looking for a signal in noise.
- **Send five direct outreach messages a week** to companies with a French gap. Short.
  Specific. Attach the teardown. Never send a generic pitch.
- After your **first five paid deliverables, raise your rate 25%.** Immediately. New
  clients only — never surprise existing ones. Most people wait a year to do this and
  it costs them a year of income.

**Expected earnings: $100–$400/month.**

---

## Weeks 9–12 — Shape a business

**Goal: stop being a freelancer for hire; start being a service with a price.**

1. **Package the productized service.** Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline —
   see [01 — The Map §2.1](01-the-map.md#21-the-productized-service). Example:
   *"Help centre localisation: up to 20 articles, publishable French, 10 working
   days — $600."*
2. **Build a one-page site.** Carrd, Notion, or plain HTML. What you do, for whom,
   at what price, with the three portfolio pieces and any testimonials. One page.
   Not a brand. Not a logo. One page.
3. **Ask every past client one question:** *"Who else do you know who needs French
   content?"* Referrals are the cheapest client acquisition that exists, and almost
   nobody asks.
4. **Start publishing once a week.** LinkedIn or X, in both languages: something
   real you learned about francophone West African markets. Not "10 tips." Actual
   observations from actual work. This is the seed of
   [§2.4](01-the-map.md#24-content-and-audience), and it takes 30 minutes.
5. **Open your first investment account** — Cowrywise, PiggyVest or Risevest — and
   move something into it. ₦10,000 counts. See
   [04 — Money That Works](04-money-that-works.md). The amount is irrelevant; the
   habit is the entire point.

**Expected earnings: $300–$800/month.**

## The 15 hours a week, allocated

| Block | Hours | What |
|---|---|---|
| Cash work | 6 | Tutoring sessions, AI training tasks |
| Moat building | 5 | Portfolio pieces, client work, outreach |
| Learning | 2 | The skill gap between where you are and $40/hr |
| Admin | 2 | Invoicing, tracking, applications, tax records |

If a week gets eaten by exams, protect the **cash work** and let the rest slide. Your
degree is not the thing you sacrifice — it's the credential behind the economics
positioning that makes the whole plan work.

---

## What to measure

Track these four weekly. Nothing else.

| Metric | Week 4 target | Week 8 | Week 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outreach sent | 10 | 40 | 80 |
| Paid deliverables completed | 0–1 | 4 | 10 |
| Money received (USD) | $0–50 | $150 | $500 |
| Effective hourly rate | — | $8 | $15 |

**The last row is the only one that really matters.** Rising income with a flat
hourly rate means you bought money with hours — that's a job. A rising *rate* means
you're building something. Watch that number like it's your GPA.

---

## The four ways this plan fails

1. **Path-switching.** Week 5 feels slow, so you abandon bilingual services for
   dropshipping, then for crypto, then for print-on-demand. Nothing compounds. This
   is the number one killer and it always disguises itself as ambition.
2. **Studying instead of shipping.** Four courses, six YouTube playlists, no client.
   You need roughly 20% of what you think you need to know before charging. Learn the
   remaining 80% while being paid.
3. **Underpricing forever.** Charging $5/hour in month nine because you're scared to
   ask. Raise your rate every five clients. It will feel too early. Do it anyway.
4. **Working without records.** No tracker, no tax set-aside, no contracts. Then a
   ₦2m year arrives with no records, and 2026's tax law is not the year to discover
   that problem.

---

## Day 91

Answer these honestly:

- Did money arrive? **How much, from where?**
- Which activity produced the highest **rate per hour** — not the most total money?
- What did clients pay for that you *expected* them to pay for? What surprised you?
- Which path do you want to be doing in three years?

Then do more of the highest-rate thing, and drop one thing entirely. The next 90 days
should be **narrower** than the last 90, not wider. Every good version of this story
gets more focused over time.
