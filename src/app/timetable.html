The 5.00 Week

Miva is asynchronous — no lecture ever forces you to be anywhere. That freedom is the whole risk, so this timetable is the structure nothing else provides. Built around three fixed points: devotion at 06:00, no coursework before 09:00, and worship at 16:00 on Sunday.

Why it is built this way

Each rule earns its place

Devotion opens every day

06:00 to 07:00, all seven days. It is the one block nothing is allowed to move, and the rest of the week is arranged around it rather than the other way round.

Coursework waits until 09:00

Your rule, kept exactly. The eight o'clock hour goes to internship applications and your own research — work that matters but isn't coursework, so the morning isn't idle and the line still holds.

Hardest work in the 09:00 block

Decision Science, Statistics and the two intermediate theory courses take the first two hours, while focus is cheapest and nothing else is competing for it yet.

Courses recur, they don't block

Each course appears two or three times across the week rather than once in a long sitting. Spacing the same material across days is the difference between recognising it and recalling it.

Recall at 16:15, not re-reading

That hour is for retrieving the morning's material from memory. Re-reading feels productive and mostly is not; retrieval is what survives to the exam hall.

LMS volume gets its own slot

GST 212 alone has 111 activities. That is throughput work, not thinking work, so it sits at 14:15 where concentration is already fading rather than stealing a deep-work block.

Trading is boxed, not banned

Two hours, three afternoons, inside the New York session. Trading is the one activity on your list whose hours expand on their own, so it gets a box with walls rather than a floor.

Religious study closes the day

20:15 on weekdays, 19:00 on Saturday — after the working day is done, six days a week. Sunday has worship at 16:00 in its place.

Hours per course

Scaled by credit units and difficulty

SSC 202 draws the most at six hours — three credit units, the largest single course on the timetable. GST 212 draws the least in deep work but the most in LMS activities: you need an efficient A there, not a brilliant one. Marks per hour is the thing to watch.

Built on a 7.25-hour night (22:15 to 05:30) and the fixed commitments estimated in the Command Center. Devotion, religious study and worship are treated as fixed, not as activities competing for leftover time. Exams begin 28 November — in crunch weeks trading and German halve and the deep-work blocks absorb them.
