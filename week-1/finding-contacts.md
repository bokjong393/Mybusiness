# Finding Businesses — and Not Losing My Number

**Correction to my own week-1 plan.** It said "send 20 WhatsApp messages." That is close to
the exact behaviour WhatsApp bans people for. Revised below.

## The rule, in one line

> **WhatsApp is not a cold outreach channel.**

Messaging people who never opted in is the **number one cause of WhatsApp bans.** Enforcement
is tiered and it ends badly: warning → messaging limits → temporary restriction → permanent
ban. A block rate above **2%** is enough to start the slide. Twenty strangers messaged in one
evening from a fresh number is a textbook spam signal.

Losing that number would cost me more than the clients are worth — it is how my family,
my school and my future customers reach me.

## What actually triggers a ban

| Trigger | Present in the original plan? |
|---|---|
| Messaging people who never opted in | **Yes** |
| High block-and-report rate | Risked |
| Sudden volume spikes from a new number | **Yes** |
| Purchased or scraped lists | No — and never will be |
| Unofficial bulk-sender tools or modified WhatsApp apps | No — and never will be |

## The revised sequence

**Instagram first. Let them bring me to WhatsApp.**

1. **First contact on Instagram DM.** Businesses publish there specifically to be contacted,
   a DM from a stranger is completely normal, and Instagram is far more tolerant of it than
   WhatsApp. My WhatsApp number stays clean.
2. **They move the conversation.** Very often the reply is *"écris-moi sur WhatsApp"* with the
   number. Now the conversation is one **they** started — an entirely different risk profile,
   and no cold message was ever sent.
3. **Or I use the link they published.** A `wa.me` link in an Instagram bio, or a WhatsApp
   button on a Facebook page, is a public invitation to message. Using it is not cold
   outreach in the way a harvested number is. Still send one personal message, never a
   template.

## Where to find businesses — ranked by quality

1. **Referrals.** One introduction outperforms fifty cold messages. Start with people who
   already know me — family trade contacts, church, campus, my mother's friends who sell.
   This is the least glamorous and highest-yielding source, and it is free.
2. **Instagram business profiles.** Search by hashtag and location — `#cotonoubusiness`,
   `#pagnebenin`, `#lagosvendor`, `#abidjanbusiness`. The signal I want is visible: comments
   asking prices with no reply, "DM for price" on every post, questions two days old.
3. **Physical signage.** In Cotonou and Lagos, shops paint their WhatsApp number on the
   storefront. An afternoon walking Dantokpa or Ganhi with a notebook costs nothing and gives
   me numbers *published for the public to use* — plus I can see how busy they are.
4. **Google Maps / Google Business Profiles.** Search the category and city, filter for
   listings with a phone number and recent reviews.
5. **Facebook Pages** with a WhatsApp button.
6. **Marketplace listings** — Jiji, Jumia sellers, local classified sites.
7. **Market associations and cooperatives.** One conversation with a market union officer
   can be worth more than the whole list.

## What I will never do

- **Harvest numbers from WhatsApp groups.** Common practice in Nigeria, and both a
  fast route to a ban and a straightforward privacy violation. Those people did not publish
  their number to me — they joined a group.
- **Buy a list.** Named ban trigger, and the numbers are worthless anyway.
- **Use bulk senders, GB WhatsApp, or any modified client.** Named ban trigger.
- **Send the same message to twenty people.** If it could be sent to anyone, it will be
  read as spam — by the recipient and by the filter.

## Protecting myself

- **Use a second SIM for business.** A cheap dedicated number. If it ever gets restricted, I
  have lost a business line, not my life. Do this before sending anything.
- **Pace it.** Three to five new conversations a day, spread out, typed individually. Not
  twenty in one evening.
- **Watch the block rate.** If people are blocking me, the message is wrong — the fix is a
  better message, never a bigger list.
- **Never paste and send.** Every message gets a detail only that business would recognise.
  That detail is also what makes it work.

## One legal note

Nigeria's Data Protection Act and Benin's data protection regime both govern how personal
data is used for marketing. Scraped numbers are personal data. Contacting a business through
a channel it published for that purpose is ordinary commerce; harvesting private numbers and
messaging them is a different thing with a different exposure. Staying on the first side of
that line is free.
