# The 40-Second Demo

One video. It replaces every explanation I would otherwise type. Film it once in week 2,
send it a hundred times.

## Set-up before filming

1. Create a WhatsApp Business account for an invented shop — **"Boutique Kelia"**, a
   fictional pagne and ready-to-wear seller in Cotonou. Invented so nobody's real business
   is misrepresented, and say so if anyone asks.
2. Write its knowledge properly. This is the actual work and the demo is only convincing if
   this part is good:
   - 8–10 real products with real prices in FCFA
   - Delivery: zones, cost, time
   - Hours, and what happens outside them
   - Payment: Mobile Money accepted, deposit rules
   - Sizes, fabric, care
   - **The handover rule:** anything about a custom order, a complaint, or a price that
     isn't listed goes straight to a human instead of guessing
3. Set the phone clock visibly to something like 23:12. The time is half the argument.

## What to film — one continuous screen recording, no cuts

| Seconds | On screen |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | The clock at 23:12. A customer types: *« Bonsoir, le pagne bleu de la photo, c'est combien et vous livrez à Calavi ? »* |
| 5–15 | The reply arrives in seconds: price, delivery cost to Calavi, delivery time, payment options |
| 15–25 | Customer: *« Vous avez ma taille ? »* — it answers, then asks if she'd like to reserve |
| 25–35 | Customer asks something unusual — *« Je peux avoir une remise pour 10 pièces ? »* — and it says it's passing this to the owner, who will reply in the morning |
| 35–40 | Cut to the owner's phone the next morning: one message waiting, already qualified |

## Why the last ten seconds matter most

Everyone demonstrating this shows the assistant answering. Almost nobody shows it **refusing
to answer.** That fifth beat is what defuses the objection every single owner will raise —
*"it will say something wrong and embarrass me"* — and it is the strongest ten seconds in the
video.

## Film it twice

Once in French, once in English. Same shop, same script. Two files, and I always send the
one that matches how they wrote to me.

## What to write when sending it

> **EN:** This is what I meant — 40 seconds. The shop is made up, but the setup is real.
>
> **FR:** Voilà ce que je voulais dire — 40 secondes. La boutique est inventée, mais
> l'installation est réelle.

## Never

- Never claim a fictional shop is a real client.
- Never film a real business's chats without written permission.
- Never edit the video to hide a wrong answer. If it answers badly, fix the knowledge and
  film again — the fixing *is* the skill I am selling.
