# Validation Experiment — Phase 9

**We are not testing whether the idea is clever. We are testing three claims, in order. If claim 1 fails, claims 2 and 3 are irrelevant.**

1. Small B2B agencies have a real, felt problem sourcing relevant prospects.
2. They care enough to spend time on it.
3. **They will hand money to an unknown vendor to fix it.**

Only claim 3 is real validation. Claims 1 and 2 are diagnostic.

---

## What does not count as evidence

| Not evidence | Why it's misleading |
|---|---|
| "Great idea!" | Costs nothing to say. People are kind to strangers. |
| LinkedIn likes | Approval of a post, not of a purchase. |
| Survey responses | People predict their behaviour badly, and generously. |
| Waitlist signups | A signup is a maybe with no cost attached. |
| "Send me more info" | Usually a polite exit. |
| A booked call that no-shows | Curiosity, not intent. |

**One $147 payment outweighs all of the above combined.** Payment is the only signal with a cost attached, and cost is what makes a signal true.

---

## The experiment

**Window:** 30 days.
**Budget:** under $40.
**Effort:** ~60 hours.

| Stage | Threshold | If below threshold |
|---|---:|---|
| Contacts sent | **200** | Nothing is being tested. Send more before concluding anything. |
| Reply rate | **≥ 5%** (10 replies) | Message or ICP wrong → see decision tree |
| Positive replies | **≥ 8** | Offer is unclear or uninteresting |
| Real conversations | **≥ 5** | Free sample isn't compelling enough |
| Serious leads (asked price or timeline) | **≥ 3** | Trust gap or wrong buyer |
| **Paid customers** | **≥ 1** | **The core claim failed** |
| **Revenue** | **≥ $147** | — |

### Success tiers

| Result | Verdict | Next |
|---|---|---|
| 2+ paid, 1 converts to retainer | **Strong** | Scale outreach immediately. Go to the 30-day plan. |
| 1 paid | **Weak pass** | Real but unproven. Run a second 200-contact cycle before changing anything. |
| 0 paid, 3+ serious leads | **Near miss** | The problem is real; the offer or trust is wrong. Fix the offer, not the market. |
| 0 paid, < 3 serious leads | **Fail** | Something upstream is wrong. Use the decision tree. |

---

## Decision tree if the experiment fails

Diagnose in this order. **Change one variable at a time** — changing the customer, offer and price simultaneously teaches you nothing.

**1. Reply rate below 3% over 200 contacts → the message is wrong.**
Rewrite the subject line and first sentence. Test 50 contacts with a radically different opener. Cost: 3 hours. *Do not conclude the business is bad from a bad subject line.*

**2. Good replies, no conversations → the free sample is too thin.**
Send 10 rows instead of 3 to the 20 warmest prospects. If that converts, the fix is proof volume. Cost: 5 hours.

**3. Conversations, no serious leads → the offer is wrong.**
Ask the five most engaged prospects directly: *"What would this need to be for you to buy it?"* Listen for a different deliverable — they may want appointments, not research. Cost: 0.

**4. Serious leads, no payment → price or trust.**
Test $97. If that closes, it was price. If not, it was trust — add the "don't pay if unusable" guarantee more prominently and lead with the payment method before they ask. Cost: 0.

**5. Nothing works across two full cycles → change the customer, not the skill.**
Same capability, different buyer, in this order:
- **Boutique recruiters** (Plan C) — higher willingness to pay, same deliverable shape
- **SMB contractors bidding on tenders** (Plan B) — higher urgency, more recurring
- **Startup founders raising** — highest urgency, but one-off

**6. Three different buyers, three failed cycles → abandon the thesis.**
That is ~90 days and ~600 contacts. At that point the evidence is real and the honest move is to stop. Record it in `FAILURES.md` with the actual numbers, and reopen `research/OPPORTUNITY-ANALYSIS.md` at rank 3.

**What would NOT justify abandoning:** one bad week, a run of rejections, feeling discouraged, or a competitor appearing. Only the numbers above justify it.

---

## The falsification test

Written before the experiment, so it cannot be rationalized afterwards:

> **If, after 200 contacts and 5 conversations, not one agency will pay $147 for a deliverable they have already seen a free sample of — then agencies do not value this work enough to buy it, and no amount of better copywriting will change that.**

Sign the date on it. Honour it.
