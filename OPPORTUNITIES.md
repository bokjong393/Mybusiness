$3,000/month bar (so willingness-to-pay and recurring revenue matter a lot), no audience

## M1 — EN⇄FR Business & Financial Document Localization

- **Customer:** Startups, consultancies, funds and NGOs operating across the anglophone/francophone West Africa line; translation agencies needing a finance-literate subcontractor.
- **Problem:** Machine translation destroys credibility in investor and donor documents. Generic translators do not know financial or economic terminology, and do not know the region. A badly translated pitch deck costs a funding round.
- **Offer:** Investor-ready localization of pitch decks, market reports, grant applications, financial statements, and web/product copy — EN⇄FR, delivered formatted and presentation-ready, not as raw text.
- **Why they pay:** The document has a deadline and money attached to it. Failure is visible and expensive.
- **Price:** $150–$600 per document; decks $250–$500; per-word $0.08–$0.15 for volume work.
- **Revenue model:** Project → repeat → monthly retainer for firms with continuous output.
- **Gross margin:** ~95% (time only; no COGS).
- **Difficulty:** Low. **Time to first revenue:** 1–3 weeks.
- **Scalability:** Medium — capped by hours until a vetted translator bench exists.
- **Competitive intensity:** High globally (translation is commoditized), **low** in the finance/economics + West Africa niche.
- **Unfair advantage:** Native bilingualism + economics vocabulary + deck-production skill is a rare triple. Most bilinguals cannot format an investor deck; most deck people are not bilingual.
- **LinkedIn acquisition:** Search titles containing expansion / country manager / partnerships across WAEMU; vendor managers at translation agencies; Africa-focused consultancies. Direct, personalised outreach — no audience required.
- **Scores:** Speed 9 · Fit 9 · WTP 8 · Ease 9 · Scale 5 · Recurring 6 · Distribution 8 · Defensibility 4 → **7.55**

## M2 — Francophone West Africa Market-Entry Research Briefs

- **Customer:** African and international companies entering Benin/Togo/Côte d'Ivoire/Senegal — and francophone companies entering Nigeria and Ghana (the reverse direction is underserved and the prize is bigger).
- **Problem:** There is no affordable, credible, English-language intelligence on WAEMU markets. Big-4 firms will not take a $2k engagement. Free information is stale, fragmented, and often French-only.
- **Offer:** Fixed-scope 15–25 page brief in 10 business days: market structure, regulatory and licensing landscape, competitor map, distribution channels, pricing norms, entry-route recommendation, named local contacts.
- **Why they pay:** Entering a market blind costs six figures. A $900 brief that prevents one bad decision is trivially justified.
- **Price:** $600–$2,500.
- **Revenue model:** Project → quarterly refresh subscription.
- **Gross margin:** 90%+.
- **Difficulty:** Medium — requires genuine research rigour and at least one public sample.
- **Time to first revenue:** 3–8 weeks.
- **Scalability:** Medium→High. Research done once can be resold as a syndicated report.
- **Competitive intensity:** Low in this exact niche.
- **Unfair advantage:** French-language primary sources plus in-region presence. An analyst in London literally cannot read half the source material.
- **LinkedIn acquisition:** Expansion leads, country managers, VC platform/portfolio roles, NGO programme managers, chambers of commerce.
- **Scores:** Speed 6 · Fit 9 · WTP 8 · Ease 6 · Scale 7 · Recurring 6 · Distribution 7 · Defensibility 8 → **7.30**

## M3 — Research-as-a-Service Retainer for Boutique Consultancies

- **Customer:** Africa-focused consultancies, market research firms, strategy boutiques, due-diligence shops, ESG/impact advisors.
- **Problem:** Demand is lumpy. They cannot justify a full-time bilingual analyst but keep losing time to desk research they should not be doing at partner rates.
- **Offer:** White-labelled desk research on retainer — N briefs or N hours per month, EN/FR, delivered to their template under their brand.
- **Why they pay:** Cheaper than a hire, faster than recruiting, and it protects billable partner hours.
- **Price:** $500–$1,500/month.
- **Revenue model:** **Recurring** — the first genuinely recurring line available without Stripe.
- **Gross margin:** 90%.
- **Difficulty:** Medium — sold on reliability and samples, not pedigree, which suits the credential gap.
- **Time to first revenue:** 4–10 weeks.
- **Scalability:** High — the natural path to an agency with junior researchers.
- **Competitive intensity:** Medium.
- **Unfair advantage:** Bilingual + overlapping timezone with Europe + regional context + cost base.
- **LinkedIn acquisition:** Research directors, principals, and founders of 2–20 person consultancies. Small firms, reachable people, decisions made by one person.
- **Scores:** Speed 5 · Fit 8 · WTP 8 · Ease 6 · Scale 8 · Recurring 9 · Distribution 7 · Defensibility 6 → **7.15**

## M4 — Donor / NGO Bilingual Research & Proposal Support

- **Customer:** NGOs, development contractors, and independent consultants bidding on donor-funded work in WAEMU countries.
- **Problem:** Donor calls demand French submissions, baseline studies and situational analyses on short deadlines; small organisations have no bilingual research capacity.
- **Offer:** Desk reviews, baseline/situation analyses, proposal translation and formatting, data summaries.
- **Price:** $400–$2,000/project.
- **Gross margin:** 90%. **Difficulty:** Medium. **Time to first revenue:** 6–12 weeks (procurement is slow).
- **Recurring:** Medium — framework agreements and repeat bid support.
- **Unfair advantage:** Bilingual + regional + fast + affordable, in a sector where those four rarely co-occur.
- **Risk:** Payment terms are slow and bureaucratic. Do not make this the first revenue line.
- **Scores:** Speed 3 · Fit 8 · WTP 8 · Ease 5 · Scale 6 · Recurring 7 · Distribution 6 · Defensibility 7 → **6.35**

## M6 — Diaspora Real Estate Feasibility & Verification Reports

- **Customer:** Nigerian and Beninese diaspora investors buying property back home; small developers.
- **Problem:** Diaspora buyers are defrauded at scale and have no independent eyes on the ground.
- **Offer:** Independent market feasibility and property/title verification report.
- **Price:** $200–$800. **Speed:** Fast. **Distribution:** Excellent — diaspora are numerous and highly reachable on LinkedIn.
- **Serious caveat:** Verification implies liability. If a buyer loses money after your report, you are exposed. Requires explicit scope limits and disclaimers, and probably a local partner for physical inspection. Does not scale internationally.
- **Scores:** Speed 7 · Fit 7 · WTP 7 · Ease 6 · Scale 4 · Recurring 3 · Distribution 7 · Defensibility 6 → **6.10**

## Reading the ranking

The top three are not competitors — they are **the same business at three stages of maturity**, sold to overlapping
buyers. That is the strongest possible result from this exercise, and it defines the ladder in BUSINESS.md.

The bottom four are all versions of the same mistake: selling to people like yourself (students, job seekers,
peers) or selling a certificate that a hundred thousand other people also hold.
