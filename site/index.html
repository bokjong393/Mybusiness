La thèse · The one idea

Online income has three layers. Most people never learn the other two exist.

They are not a menu you choose from. They are a sequence: hours fund the system, the system funds the assets, and the assets are the thing you actually want.

Trois coups à jouer · Where to actually start

Your French is worth more than your English

Everyone competing with you writes English. Very few write publishable French and understand West African business from the inside. Benin, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Togo, Cameroon — plus France and Canada — is a large, well-funded, badly served market, and you were born into both halves of it.

MOVE 01 · NOW

Localisation for African fintech

Nigerian and Kenyan companies are pushing into francophone West Africa and need product copy, help-centre articles and support scripts in French that don't read like machine translation. Recurring work, not one-off gigs.

$12–22/hr starting → $30–50 specialised
MOVE 02 · NOW

Market-entry research briefs

"How does mobile-money pricing differ between Benin and Côte d'Ivoire?" is a question companies pay real money to have answered by someone who reads French sources and understands the economics. Your degree stops being a piece of paper here.

$300–1,500 per deliverable
MOVE 03 · MONTH 3

Package it as one product

Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline: "Help-centre localisation — up to 20 articles, publishable French, 10 working days." Quoting takes five minutes, delivery becomes a checklist, and a checklist is something you can eventually hand to someone else.

$400–1,500 per engagement

Arnaques · Currently hunting you

₦316 billion lost to documented Ponzi schemes

The CBEX collapse alone cost Nigerian investors an estimated $1 billion in 2025. These were not stupid people. They were ordinary people shown a very good story — an unsolicited recruiter DM, a guaranteed return, a deepfaked celebrity endorsement, a ₦25,000 fee to "unlock" higher earnings.

Four questions, asked before any money moves

  1. Did I go to them, or did they come to me? Inbound offers are many times more likely to be fraudulent. The biggest job scam in Nigeria right now is a WhatsApp message from a recruiter you never contacted.
  2. Am I being asked for money in order to earn money? Training fees, registration fees, equipment deposits, "certification." Legitimate employers do not charge you to work. There is no exception to this rule.
  3. Is the return guaranteed? Then it is a lie. Real returns are uncertain by definition — that is what makes them returns.
  4. Does it pay me to recruit other people? Then I am the product, and the people I bring in are the payout.

Everything legitimate on this page takes months and pays ordinary amounts of money. Anything promising to beat that timeline is selling you the story — and you are the revenue.

Les 90 premiers jours · The plan

Stack a fast cash path on a slow moat path

Cash arrives from tutoring and AI training work while the valuable thing — bilingual services for African fintech — is being built. Do not add a third path. The failure mode is never laziness; it is six half-finished things.

WEEKS 1–2

Foundations

  • Open Payoneer, plus Grey or Raenest. Verification takes days — do it before a client is waiting.
  • Open a separate bank account for business money only.
  • Start an income tracker: date, client, amount, currency, fees, naira value.
  • Write a one-page profile in English and French.
  • Apply to two AI-training platforms; create a Preply or italki tutor profile.
Expected: ₦0 — and that is correct
WEEKS 3–4

Proof without clients

  • Pick three real companies with a French-language gap.
  • Localise a real page of theirs properly, as if paid — pricing page, onboarding email, three help articles.
  • Write a one-page teardown for each: what's broken, what you'd fix, what it costs them in signups.
  • Send each teardown to their growth lead. Most won't reply. One reply is all you need.
Expected: $0–80
WEEKS 5–8

First real money

  • Three to five tutoring sessions a week. Ask every satisfied student for a written review.
  • Apply to 8–12 bilingual gigs a week; send five direct outreach messages with the teardown attached.
  • After your first five paid deliverables, raise your rate 25% — new clients only. It will feel too early. Do it anyway.
Expected: $100–400/month
WEEKS 9–12

Shape a business

  • Package the productized service — fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline.
  • Build one page: what you do, for whom, at what price, with three portfolio pieces. One page, not a brand.
  • Ask every past client: "Who else do you know who needs French content?"
  • Publish once a week in both languages — something real you learned, not "10 tips."
  • Open your first investment account and move ₦10,000 into it. The amount is irrelevant; the habit is the point.
Expected: $300–800/month
The only metric that really matters is your effective hourly rate. Rising income at a flat rate means you bought money with hours — that is a job. A rising rate means you are building something. Watch that number like it's your GPA.
Faire travailler l'argent · Layer three

What to do with the money once it arrives

Two forces work against naira savings at once: depreciation against anything priced in dollars, and inflation at home. So the goal is not "save money" — it is to hold assets that grow faster than your currency loses value, and to hold some of them in a currency that isn't yours.
